## One-line summary

Write the improved policy as a product $$\pi(a \mid s) \propto \hat{\pi}(a \mid s)\, f(A(s, a))$$ of the reference policy and a non-negative, increasing function of advantage. Two facts: any such product is a policy improvement, and raising $$f$$ to a higher power improves it further. Diffusion and flow models can sample products of distributions by adding scores, and classifier-free guidance (CFG) does exactly that with a single network that is trained both with and without an "optimality" conditioning bit. So CFGRL trains one conditional flow policy by ordinary supervised learning and, at sampling time, dials the guidance weight $$w$$ to choose how much to improve over the data. It beats advantage-weighted regression in offline RL, and it turns goal-conditioned behavioral cloning into something better without training any value function.

## Why it matters

The previous three papers make diffusion models into RL policies by differentiating through the sampler. CFGRL points in the opposite direction: keep the training a supervised regression, and put the RL into the *sampler* through guidance. This is the idea that Physical Intelligence then scales up to a real robot VLA in [π*0.6 / RECAP](/blog/pi-star-06-recap/), and it explains what DiffusionNFT's "reinforcement guidance" is generalizing.

## Setup (Section 3)

Standard MDP with discounted objective $$J(\pi_{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau}\big[\sum_t \gamma^t r(s_t, a_t)\big]$$. For a reference policy $$\hat{\pi}$$ define $$V^{\hat{\pi}}, Q^{\hat{\pi}}$$ and the advantage $$A^{\hat{\pi}}(s, a) = Q^{\hat{\pi}}(s, a) - V^{\hat{\pi}}(s)$$. The classic result: a new policy $$\pi$$ with non-negative expected advantage under its own state distribution,

$$
\mathbb{E}_{(s, a) \sim p_{\pi}}\big[A^{\hat{\pi}}(s, a)\big] \ge 0
$$

improves on $$\hat{\pi}$$. Practical algorithms maximize the surrogate under the *reference's* states, $$\tilde{J}(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{s \sim p_{\hat{\pi}}}\mathbb{E}_{a \sim \pi}[A^{\hat{\pi}}(s, a)]$$, which bounds the true objective when the two policies stay close, hence the KL-penalized objective

$$
J(\pi_{\theta}) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau}\Big[\sum_t \gamma^t r(s_t, a_t)\Big] - \beta\, \mathbb{E}_{s}\big[D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot \mid s)\,\Vert\,\hat{\pi}(\cdot \mid s))\big]
$$

## Product policies improve (Section 4)

Parameterize the new policy as

$$
\pi(a \mid s) \propto \hat{\pi}(a \mid s)\, f\big(A(s, a)\big)
$$

- **Remark 1.** If $$f$$ is non-negative and monotonically increasing in $$A^{\hat{\pi}}$$, then $$\pi$$ is an improvement over $$\hat{\pi}$$ (proof in the appendix; it generalizes bandit and exponentiated-advantage results).
- **Remark 2.** For $$0 \le w_1 < w_2$$ and $$\pi_{w} \propto \hat{\pi}\, f(A)^{w}$$, $$\pi_{w_2}$$ improves over $$\pi_{w_1}$$. Bigger exponent, more improvement, but also more deviation from the reference, and out-of-distribution actions can hurt empirically.
- **Remark 3.** The maximizer of the KL-penalized objective above is itself a product policy, $$\pi(a \mid s) \propto \hat{\pi}(a \mid s)\exp(A(s, a))^{1/\beta}$$. Advantage-weighted regression (AWR) fits this directly, but $$\beta$$ is baked into the training objective. CFGRL keeps the two factors separate so the exponent can be chosen at test time.

## Composing the factors with guidance (Section 4.1)

Introduce a binary optimality variable $$o \in \{\varnothing, 0, 1\}$$ with $$p(o = 1 \mid s, a) = f(A(s, a)) / Z(s)$$ (the normalizer is never needed). Then the product policy is $$\pi(a \mid s) \propto \hat{\pi}(a \mid s)\, p(o \mid s, a)$$, and because diffusion models work with scores, which add under products,

$$
\nabla_a \log \pi(a \mid s) = \nabla_a \log \hat{\pi}(a \mid s) + \nabla_a \log p(o \mid s, a)
$$

Learning an explicit optimality classifier $$p(o \mid s, a)$$ is unattractive (normalization, adversarial gradients on noised actions). Bayes' rule turns it into the difference of two *policy* scores, which is the classifier-free guidance identity:

$$
\nabla_a \log \pi(a \mid s) = \nabla_a \log \hat{\pi}(a \mid s) + \big(\nabla_a \log \hat{\pi}(a \mid s, o) - \nabla_a \log \hat{\pi}(a \mid s)\big)
$$

Both terms are the same conditional policy, with and without the conditioning bit, so one network suffices. Add a guidance weight:

$$
\nabla_a \log \hat{\pi}(a \mid s) + w\big(\nabla_a \log \hat{\pi}(a \mid s, o) - \nabla_a \log \hat{\pi}(a \mid s)\big) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \pi(a \mid s) \propto \hat{\pi}(a \mid s)\, p(o \mid s, a)^{w} = \hat{\pi}(a \mid s)\, f(A)^{w}
$$

By Remark 2, increasing $$w$$ at sampling time increases policy improvement. The regularization–optimality trade-off that offline RL normally sweeps by retraining becomes a single inference-time scalar.

## Training and sampling (Section 4.2, Algorithms 1–2)

Flow matching with a velocity network $$v_{\theta}(a_t, t, s, o)$$: sample $$(s, a) \sim \mathcal{D}$$, noise $$a_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$$, $$t \sim U(0, 1)$$, label $$o \in \{0, 1\}$$, drop the label to $$\varnothing$$ with probability 0.1, and regress

$$
L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{s, a \sim \mathcal{D}}\big[\lVert v_{\theta}(a_t, t, s, o) - (a - a_0)\rVert^2\big], \qquad a_t = (1 - t)a_0 + t\, a
$$

No weighting term anywhere. Sampling integrates $$v = (1 - w)\, v_{\theta}(a, t, s, \varnothing) + w\, v_{\theta}(a, t, s, o = 1)$$ for $$N$$ Euler steps. (Flow networks output velocity rather than score, but prior work shows guidance behaves the same in practice.)

## Application 1: policy extraction in offline RL (Section 5)

Learn $$Q, V$$ with implicit Q-learning, then extract a policy. AWR maximizes $$\mathbb{E}[\log \pi_{\theta}(a \mid s)\exp(A(s, a)/\beta)]$$; its weights become peaked, so a handful of samples dominate each batch and most data is ignored (Figure 2, left). CFGRL uses the simplest optimality rule, $$o = \mathbb{1}[A(s, a) \ge 0]$$, which is non-negative and non-decreasing as Remark 1 requires, and trains with even weights (Figure 2, right).

- **Guidance vs temperature (Figure 3).** On walker-run and cheetah-run, AWR saturates around $$1/\beta = 10$$; CFGRL keeps improving as $$w$$ grows, to a higher maximum.
- **ExORL (Table 1).** CFGRL beats AWR on 8 of 9 tasks, e.g. walker-stand 603 → 782, cheetah-run-backward 146 → 262.
- **OGBench (Table 2).** Mixed but mostly favorable (pointmaze-teleport 3 → 57; antmaze-large 50 → 20 is a loss).

## Application 2: goal-conditioned BC for free (Section 6)

In goal-conditioned RL with hindsight relabeling, GCBC trains $$\pi(a \mid s, g)$$ on actions that eventually reached $$g$$. The paper shows GCBC is *implicitly* the CFGRL policy at $$w = 1$$: conditioning on the goal is conditioning on optimality. So the same network, sampled with $$w > 1$$ (guiding from the unconditional or goal-agnostic policy toward the goal-conditioned one), extrapolates beyond GCBC with no value function. Across state-based, visual and hierarchical OGBench tasks (Table 3, Figure 4) success rates rise consistently, sometimes doubling.

## Thoughts

- The theorem is the paper: "product of reference and increasing function of advantage improves" plus "CFG samples products". Everything else follows, including why the AWR temperature and the CFG weight play the same role but the latter is free to sweep.
- Training is a pure regression with even weights, which is why it scales; the RL is entirely in the sampler. That is a very different design philosophy from DDPO/DPPO/Flow-GRPO, and the robotics community adopted it fast.
- The limits are honest: large $$w$$ extrapolates off the data manifold, and with a hard threshold $$A \ge 0$$ the method still needs a critic in the offline-RL case. π*0.6 later swaps the test-time $$w$$ for a training-time advantage threshold for exactly this reason.
