## One-line summary

Large, heterogeneous robot data (many robots and strategies, failures, autonomous rollouts from RL-trained models, human egocentric video, web data) is only useful if the model can tell the episodes apart. π0.7 does that by putting *how* the episode was performed into the prompt: the sub-task in detailed language, one or more **subgoal images**, **episode metadata** (length in steps, a 1–5 quality score, a mistake flag) and the **control mode**, each dropped out at random during training. At test time the prompt is composed to ask for fast, high-quality, mistake-free behaviour, with subgoals generated by a lightweight world model and sub-tasks from a high-level policy or a human coach. The result is a 5B VLA that performs dexterous multi-stage tasks out of the box at the level of RL-fine-tuned specialists from [π*0.6](/blog/pi-star-06-recap/), follows 3–6-step open-ended instructions in unseen kitchens and bedrooms, transfers shirt folding zero-shot to a bimanual UR5e that never saw laundry (matching expert teleoperators' first attempts), and learns new long-horizon tasks from verbal coaching alone.

## Why it matters

The VLA series so far scaled data and model size and, in π*0.6, added RL. π0.7 argues that the missing ingredient for compositional generalization is *context*: annotate what makes each episode good or bad and let the model condition on it, the same way image generators use prompt expansion. The recipe also closes a loop with the previous paper, since RL-specialist rollouts become training data that the generalist can distill through the metadata.

## Flow-based VLAs and the training objective (Section III)

Observations $$o_t = [I^{1}_t, \dots, I^{n}_t, q_t]$$, action chunks $$a_{t:t+H}$$ from an action expert trained with flow matching, and the Knowledge Insulation recipe (FAST tokens supervise the VLM; the expert attends to the backbone but its gradients do not flow into it). Each example carries a context $$C_t$$, conventionally just an instruction $$\ell_t$$. π0.7 enlarges $$C_t$$ and maximizes the approximate likelihood

$$
\max_{\theta}\ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}}\big[\log \pi_{\theta}(a_{t:t+H} \mid o_{t-T:t}, C_t)\big]
$$

where $$\mathcal{D}$$ now deliberately includes failed and suboptimal episodes. With a rich enough $$C_t$$ these do not average into the model's default behaviour; they become conditioned-on examples.

## The prompt (Section V, Figure 3)

- **Sub-task instructions** $$\hat{\ell}_t$$ ("open the fridge door") beneath the task $$\ell_t$$ ("clean the kitchen"), as in π0.5; at runtime from a learned high-level policy or a human. Because the model follows diverse language, a person can **coach** it through a new task step by step, and the coaching transcripts can then train the high-level policy so the task becomes autonomous.
- **Subgoal images** $$g_t = [G^{1}_t, \dots, G^{n}_t]$$, one per camera (base view for object outcomes, wrist views for gripper outcomes). At runtime they come from a **world model** $$g_{\psi}$$, a 14B BAGEL image-editing model fine-tuned with the flow-matching loss

$$
\max_{\psi}\ \mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{D}_g}\big[\mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}}\big(g^{\star}_t,\ g_{\psi}(o_t, \hat{\ell}_t, m)\big)\big], \qquad g^{\star}_t = o_{t_{\text{end}}}
$$

on well-labelled segments (target = the segment's final frame), augmented with web and human video so that semantic and physical knowledge from those sources reach π0.7 through the images.
- **Episode metadata** $$m$$: overall speed (episode length binned to 500-step buckets), overall quality (1–5, human-annotated), and a per-segment mistake flag. The data's diversity in speed and quality is what lets the model correlate the labels with the actions; at runtime it is asked for the 15th-percentile length, quality 5, mistake false.
- **Control mode** $$c \in \{\text{joint}, \text{ee}\}$$.

A full prompt looks like: multi-view observation, multi-view subgoals, "Task: peel vegetables. Subtask: pick up the peeler. Speed: 8000. Quality: 5. Mistake: false. Control Mode: joint.", proprioception. **Dropout**: subgoal images are attached to only 25% of examples (with them the problem becomes inverse dynamics and trains much faster); among those, the sub-task text is dropped 30% of the time; metadata is dropped entirely 15% of the time and each field 5% individually; control mode is never dropped.

## Model and training (Section VI)

A Gemma 3 4B VLM (with its 400M vision encoder) plus a MEM-style history encoder that compresses up to six past frames per camera (1 s stride, dropped entirely with probability 0.3) to the token count of one frame, and an 860M flow-matching action expert with adaptive RMSNorm for the flow timestep, 50 action tokens with bidirectional attention. Block-causal masking: observation and subgoal tokens attend bidirectionally within themselves, subgoals also attend to observations, text is causal. Proprioceptive states (including history) are embedded by a linear projection, one token per history step. Training-time **real-time chunking** simulates 0–12 steps of inference delay (240 ms at 50 Hz). Subgoal images for training are a mix of real future frames (25% end-of-segment, 75% uniformly 0–4 s ahead) and world-model generations, to close the real–generated gap. Data: many robot platforms' demonstrations, lower-quality demonstrations and failures, autonomous evaluation data including π*0.6 RL rollouts (generalization-evaluation tasks excluded), egocentric human video, multimodal web data and video captioning.

## Prompting at runtime (Section VII, Algorithm 1)

Always provide control mode and metadata. Sub-task from the high-level policy or coach; subgoals refreshed whenever $$\hat{\ell}$$ changes or every $$\Delta = 4$$ s, generated asynchronously. Five denoising steps per 50-step chunk, executing $$\hat{H} \in \{15, 25\}$$ steps with RTC. Because every prompt component was trained with dropout, **classifier-free guidance** can be applied to any of them; each denoising step follows

$$
\nabla_a \log \pi_{\theta}(a_{t:t+H} \mid o_t, C_t) + \beta\big(\nabla_a \log \pi_{\theta}(a_{t:t+H} \mid o_t, C_t) - \nabla_a \log \pi_{\theta}(a_{t:t+H} \mid o_t, C^{\text{uncond}}_t)\big)
$$

with the metadata dropped in $$C^{\text{uncond}}_t$$ and moderate $$\beta \in \{1.3, 1.7, 2.2\}$$ to push toward faster, cleaner behaviour, the same guidance-as-improvement idea as [CFGRL](/blog/cfgrl/), here applied to a quality label rather than an advantage.

## Results (Section IX)

- **Out of the box (Figure 6).** On the π*0.6 tasks (two laundry variants, espresso, box building), the single π0.7 model matches the RL specialists' success and normalized throughput, exceeding them in diverse laundry and box building; on six "Robot Olympics" tasks it matches SFT specialists. Ablations (Figure 7): removing metadata or removing autonomous evaluation data hurts across the board, most in throughput. Memory tasks (Figure 8) match fine-tuned MEM specialists.
- **Instruction following (Figures 9–11).** Fourteen scenarios of 3–6 open-ended steps in four unseen kitchens and two unseen bedrooms: far above π0.5 and π0.6. Complex referential instructions ("pick up the object I would use to eat soup") improve further with generated subgoals. It can act *against* dataset bias ("Reverse Bussing", "Reverse Fridge to Microwave"), where subgoal images are critical.
- **Cross-embodiment (Figures 12–13).** Zero-shot transfer of towel and T-shirt folding from small static bimanual robots to the heavier, longer bimanual UR5e: 85.6% task progress and 80% success vs 90.9% / 80.6% for expert teleoperators on their own first UR5e attempts. The policy invents embodiment-appropriate strategies (single-arm bag insertion, vertical instead of tilted grasps).
- **Compositional tasks (Figures 14–17).** New short-horizon tasks (scoop rice, press a French press, spin a gear set) work by prompting; new long-horizon tasks (load or unload an air fryer, toast a bagel) work through coaching, and a high-level policy trained on the coaching transcripts reproduces them autonomously.
- **Scaling (Figure 18).** With metadata, performance keeps improving as lower-quality data is added; without metadata it degrades. Removing the most task-diverse robot data hurts compositional generalization substantially.

## Thoughts

- The core claim is about *data*, not architecture: mixed-quality episodes are an asset if the model is told how they differ. Figure 18 is the cleanest evidence I have seen that metadata conditioning is what turns "more data" into "better policy".
- Subgoal images are the most interesting mechanism: a world model with web-scale priors converts language into a concrete target state, and the VLA becomes an inverse-dynamics solver between now and that state. That is how web knowledge enters the controller without co-training on web text.
- Coaching is a new kind of data collection: no teleoperation, just talking, then distilling the talk into the high-level policy. It is also where the next paper's unified world model wants to go: one model that reasons, generates subgoal video, and acts.
