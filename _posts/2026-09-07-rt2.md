## One-line summary

Take a large vision-language model pretrained on the web (PaLI-X 55B or PaLM-E 12B), express each robot action as a string of integer tokens, and fine-tune the VLM on the RT-1 robot data *mixed with its original web data*. The resulting **vision-language-action** model keeps the VLM's knowledge and uses it for control: it moves objects onto a picture of Taylor Swift, picks the "extinct animal" toy, and reasons about which object is a makeshift hammer, none of which appear in the robot data. Generalization to unseen objects, backgrounds and environments roughly doubles over RT-1.

## Why it matters

[RT-1](/blog/rt1/) learned only from robot data. RT-2 shows that a VLM's semantic knowledge transfers to actions if actions are made to look like language. That is the definition of a VLA, and OpenVLA and π0 are direct descendants.

## Actions as text (Section 3.1)

An action is the RT-1 7-dimensional arm command plus a terminate flag: `terminate, Δx, Δy, Δz, Δroll, Δpitch, Δyaw, gripper`. Each continuous dimension is discretized into **256 bins**, and the action becomes a string of eight integers, for example

`1 128 91 241 5 101 127 217`

How integers become tokens depends on the VLM. PaLI-X already has a unique token for every integer up to 1000, so the bin indices are used as-is. PaLM-E does not, so the **256 least frequently used tokens** of its vocabulary are overwritten to represent the 256 bins. Either way, the robot data becomes ordinary (image, text) → text examples: the instruction as the question, the action string as the answer.

**Constrained decoding.** At inference the model is only allowed to emit valid action tokens; sampling is restricted to the action vocabulary so the output always parses.

**Co-fine-tuning.** Fine-tuning on robot data alone makes the VLM forget web knowledge and generalize worse. RT-2 instead trains on a mixture of the original VLM data (VQA, captioning) and robot episodes, with the robot fraction increased over training. Table / Section 5.4 ablations: co-fine-tuning beats fine-tuning by a wide margin on generalization, and both beat training from scratch (which fails completely at this model size with this little data).

**Serving.** A 55B model cannot run on the robot; RT-2 runs on a multi-TPU cloud service and is queried over the network at 1–3 Hz. The paper notes this, not model quality, is the bottleneck for deployment.

## Chain-of-thought with actions (Section 5.5)

For PaLM-E-based RT-2, the target is augmented with a short *plan* before the action: "Instruction: I'm hungry. Plan: pick rxbar chocolate. Action: 1 128 …". A few hundred such examples let the model answer instructions that require a reasoning step (choosing a drink for someone tired, an improvised tool), producing the plan in natural language and then the action tokens. This is chain-of-thought prompting from the language series, applied to control.

## Results (Section 5)

Real Everyday Robots arm, 6k evaluation trials.

- **Seen tasks (Figure 4 left):** RT-2 matches RT-1 (about 90%); no loss from moving to a VLM.
- **Generalization (Figure 4 right, Figure 3 scenarios):** on unseen objects, unseen backgrounds and unseen environments, RT-2-PaLI-X-55B reaches **62%** average vs 32% for RT-1 and the best prior (RT-1, MOO, VC-1, R3M) baselines; both RT-2 variants are 2× better than RT-1.
- **Emergent skills (Figure 6a, Figure 2):** three groups of new evaluations, symbol understanding (move the apple to "3", to the picture of a cat), reasoning (pick the object that is different, the one used as a hammer), and human recognition (move the coke can to the person with glasses): RT-2 averages about 3× RT-1's success.
- **Size and training ablations (Figure 6b):** 55B beats 5B on emergent skills; co-fine-tuning beats fine-tuning; from-scratch is near zero.
- **Language Table (Figure 5, Table 3):** on the simulated Language-Table benchmark RT-2-PaLI-3B reaches 90% vs 74% for RT-1, and in the real setup it follows out-of-distribution instructions ("push the blue cube to the letter Y") that only make sense with web knowledge.

## Thoughts

- "Actions as another language" is a trick about *interfaces*, not architectures: the VLM is untouched, only its vocabulary is reinterpreted. That is why the idea ported straight to open models (OpenVLA).
- The cost is precision and speed: 256 bins per axis, one action per forward pass of a 55B model, 1–3 Hz. Octo's diffusion head and π0's flow-matching head exist to fix exactly this.
- The co-fine-tuning result is the robotics version of InstructGPT's pretraining-mix term: keep the original distribution in the batch or lose what pretraining gave you.
