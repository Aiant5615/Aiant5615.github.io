## One-line summary

Fine-tuning changes a weight matrix $$W_0$$ by some $$\Delta W$$; assume $$\Delta W$$ has low rank, write it as $$BA$$ with two thin matrices, and train only $$A$$ and $$B$$ while $$W_0$$ stays frozen. For GPT-3 175B this cuts trainable parameters by 10,000× and GPU memory by 3× with no loss in quality and, unlike adapters, no extra inference latency because $$BA$$ can be added into $$W_0$$ after training.

## Why it matters

Full fine-tuning of a 175B model needs a separate 350GB copy per task. LoRA makes task-specific weights a few megabytes, made adapting open models on one GPU routine, and is the base of QLoRA (Part 8), which pushes the same idea onto quantized weights.

## The method

**Parametrization.** For a pretrained matrix $$W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times k}$$, constrain its update to a low-rank product,

$$
h = W_0 x + \Delta W x = W_0 x + B A x, \qquad B \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times r},\ A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times k},\ r \ll \min(d, k)
$$

Both branches see the same input; their outputs are summed. $$A$$ is initialized with a random Gaussian and **$$B$$ with zeros**, so $$\Delta W = 0$$ at the start and training begins exactly at the pretrained model. The update is scaled by $$\alpha / r$$; with Adam, tuning $$\alpha$$ is roughly equivalent to tuning the learning rate, so $$\alpha$$ is set once and not swept when $$r$$ changes.

**No inference overhead.** After training, compute $$W = W_0 + BA$$ once and use it as a normal dense layer; switching tasks means subtracting one $$BA$$ and adding another. Adapter layers, by contrast, add sequential computation that cannot be merged and cost up to 30% latency at batch size 1 (Table 1 / Figure 5 in the appendix).

**Generalization of fine-tuning.** With $$r$$ equal to the full rank, LoRA recovers full fine-tuning; with small $$r$$ it is a constrained version. It also composes with prefix tuning and adapters.

**Where to apply it.** In the Transformer, LoRA is applied to the attention projections ($$W_q, W_k, W_v, W_o$$); the MLP is left frozen in the experiments. Table 5: with a fixed budget of 18M trainable parameters on GPT-3, adapting $$W_q$$ and $$W_v$$ with $$r = 4$$ beats adapting $$W_q$$ alone with $$r = 8$$; spreading a small rank over more matrices is better than a large rank on one.

**How small can $$r$$ be?** Table 6: on GPT-3, $$r = 1$$ or $$2$$ on $$\{W_q, W_v\}$$ is already competitive on WikiSQL and MultiNLI; $$r = 8$$ is as good as $$r = 64$$. The update really does have a tiny "intrinsic rank".

## Why low rank works (Section 7)

- **Subspace similarity (Figure 3).** Train LoRA with $$r = 8$$ and $$r = 64$$ on the same task and compare the subspaces spanned by the top singular vectors of $$A$$: the top directions of the $$r = 8$$ and $$r = 64$$ solutions overlap strongly (normalized similarity near 1 for the first few directions), while the remaining directions of $$r = 64$$ look like noise. Increasing $$r$$ mostly adds directions that do not matter.
- **Across seeds (Figure 4).** Two $$r = 64$$ runs with different seeds share their top directions for $$\Delta W_q$$, and the shared count is larger than for a pair of random Gaussian matrices, so the learned subspace is a property of the task, not of the run.
- **Relation to $$W$$ (Table 7).** Projecting $$W$$ onto $$\Delta W$$'s top singular directions shows that $$\Delta W$$ *amplifies* directions that are present but not emphasized in $$W$$, by a factor of about 20 for $$r = 4$$, rather than repeating $$W$$'s dominant directions: adaptation strengthens task-relevant features the pretrained model already has.

## Results

- **RoBERTa base/large and DeBERTa XXL on GLUE (Table 2):** LoRA matches or exceeds full fine-tuning with 0.3M–4.7M trainable parameters, and beats adapters, BitFit and prefix methods at similar budgets.
- **GPT-2 medium/large on E2E, WebNLG, DART (Table 3):** LoRA beats full fine-tuning and adapters on BLEU/METEOR with 0.35M parameters.
- **GPT-3 175B (Table 4, Figure 2):** WikiSQL 73.4%, MultiNLI 91.7%, SAMSum ROUGE-1 53.8, above full fine-tuning (73.8 / 89.5 / 52.0) with 4.7M trainable parameters. Figure 2 shows accuracy vs trainable parameters for several methods: prefix tuning *degrades* past a point (more prefix tokens hurt), while LoRA is flat and high across three orders of magnitude.

## Thoughts

- Zero-initializing $$B$$ is the same trick as Flamingo's zero gates and ResNet's near-identity blocks: start as the identity, let training decide.
- The empirical claim that adaptation is low-rank is stronger than "few parameters suffice"; it is that the *update matrix* has few important directions, which is why LoRA can be merged and why later variants (DoRA, rank-stabilized LoRA) fiddle with scale rather than rank.
- LoRA changes weights, not activations; it does nothing for the memory of the frozen weights themselves. QLoRA (Part 8) attacks that half.
