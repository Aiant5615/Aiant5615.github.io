## One-line summary

Keep the [LLaVA](/blog/llava/) recipe, make four small changes (a two-layer MLP projector instead of a linear one, CLIP ViT-L/14 at 336 px, about 600K academic VQA examples added with short-answer format prompts, and Vicuna-13B), and get state of the art on 11 benchmarks with 1.2M public training samples and about one day of training on a single 8×A100 node. Also show a cheap way to handle higher resolution by splitting the image into a grid.

## Why it matters

Between LLaVA and this paper, several models (InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL) had reported better numbers on academic benchmarks using hundreds of millions of samples, Q-Formers or resamplers. LLaVA-1.5 shows a controlled study that the simple architecture was never the problem, only the data and resolution, which reset the baseline for open multimodal models.

## The changes and what each is worth (Table 1)

1. **Response-format prompting.** LLaVA answered VQA questions with long text and scored badly on short-answer benchmarks. Appending a single instruction to the question, "Answer the question using a single word or phrase", lets the same model produce either style on demand, and fixes the format mismatch that had cost dozens of points (InstructBLIP had used a more complex scheme that overfit to short answers).
2. **MLP vision-language connector.** Replace $$W$$ with a two-layer MLP with GELU. Consistent gains across benchmarks; the paper notes this mirrors the self-supervised learning finding that a nonlinear projection head helps.
3. **Academic task data.** Add VQAv2, GQA, OKVQA, A-OKVQA, OCR-VQA, TextCaps, RefCOCO and Visual Genome (region descriptions and grounding, formatted as text with normalized box coordinates). About 665K instruction samples in total including the original 158K and ShareGPT text-only conversations (which help language ability).
4. **Scaling.** 336-px input (576 visual tokens instead of 256), Vicuna-13B instead of 7B, and a wider set of data. Each step helps; the final model is LLaVA-1.5.

Training is still two stages: projector-only pretraining on the 558K LCS-558K caption subset, then full fine-tuning of projector and LLM for one epoch; 3.5 hours for 7B, 26 hours for 13B on 8 A100s.

## LLaVA-1.5-HD (Figure 2)

CLIP is trained at a fixed resolution. To use higher-resolution inputs without retraining it, split the image into a grid of crops the encoder *can* handle, encode each crop independently, and concatenate the resulting feature maps; add a downsampled *global* view of the whole image encoded separately so the LLM keeps the overall context. Any resolution and aspect ratio becomes a variable number of visual tokens. This "AnyRes"-style scheme lifts fine-detail tasks (TextVQA, and hallucination as measured by POPE) at the cost of more tokens, and is the ancestor of dynamic-resolution encoders such as Qwen2-VL's.

## Ablations (Figures 3–4, Table 2)

- **LLM choice (Figure 3).** Radar plot over 11 benchmarks for Vicuna-1.1, 1.3, 1.5 and LLaMA-2-Chat: better instruction-tuned LLMs give better multimodal models, and LLaMA-2-Chat's stronger safety alignment slightly hurts.
- **Data efficiency (Figure 4).** Training on random 10%–100% subsets: 50% of the data keeps about 98% of full performance, and even 10% is competitive, so the recipe is *sample-efficient*.
- **Hallucination.** Higher resolution lowers hallucination on POPE, as the model can see the detail it previously guessed; the paper also finds that training data with unnaturally long answers can induce hallucination.
- **Compositional generalization.** Training on long-form language reasoning (ShareGPT) improves multilingual and format-following ability on visual tasks, e.g. answering in Chinese despite no Chinese multimodal data.

## Results

State of the art on 11 of 12 benchmarks in Table 3–4 (VQAv2 80.0, GQA 63.3, TextVQA 61.3, MMBench 67.7, SEED 61.6, MM-Vet 35.4, POPE 85.9 for the 13B model), above Qwen-VL-Chat and InstructBLIP with far less data; behind on VizWiz and Qwen-VL on OCR-heavy tasks where higher-resolution encoders win.

## Thoughts

- The biggest single change is a *prompt string*. Benchmark gaps between multimodal models were, to a large extent, output-format mismatches.
- The grid trick decouples resolution from the encoder and shifts the cost to the LLM's context length; the next paper makes the encoder itself resolution-agnostic instead.
- "Simple architecture + right data" is the paper's stance, and the open-source community adopted it wholesale; nearly every 2024 open VLM is a LLaVA-1.5 variant.
