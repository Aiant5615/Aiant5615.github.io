## One-line summary

Train a family of 7B–65B-parameter decoder-only models on 1.0–1.4 trillion tokens of publicly available text, deliberately *past* the Chinchilla-optimal point because a smaller model trained longer is cheaper to serve. LLaMA-13B outperforms GPT-3 175B on most benchmarks and LLaMA-65B is competitive with Chinchilla-70B and PaLM-540B, and the weights were released to researchers, which started the open-model ecosystem.

## Why it matters

Every earlier paper in this series is a component; LLaMA is the assembly. Its architecture choices (RMSNorm pre-normalization, SwiGLU, RoPE) became the default for Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek and others, and "train small models on many tokens" became the standard scaling strategy.

## Data (Table 1)

1.4T tokens, all public: English CommonCrawl 2017–2020 (67%, deduplicated at line level, language-identified, filtered with a classifier for Wikipedia-reference-like pages), C4 (15%), GitHub (4.5%, permissive licenses, boilerplate removed), Wikipedia in 20 languages (4.5%), Gutenberg and Books3 (4.5%), arXiv (2.5%, LaTeX with preambles and bibliographies stripped), StackExchange (2%). Byte-pair encoding via SentencePiece with digits split into individual characters. Most sources are seen once; Wikipedia and books about twice.

## Architecture: three changes to the Transformer decoder

**Pre-normalization with RMSNorm.** Normalize the *input* of each sub-layer (as in GPT-2/3) rather than the output, and drop the mean-centering of LayerNorm:

$$
\operatorname{RMSNorm}(x) = \frac{x}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d} x_i^2 + \epsilon}} \odot g
$$

with a learned gain $$g$$. Cheaper than LayerNorm and, in Zhang & Sennrich's experiments, equally effective; the scale-only invariance is what matters.

**SwiGLU feed-forward.** Replace the ReLU MLP by a gated linear unit with the Swish activation (as in PaLM):

$$
\operatorname{FFN}_{\text{SwiGLU}}(x) = \big(\operatorname{Swish}_1(x W_1) \odot x V\big) W_2, \qquad \operatorname{Swish}_1(z) = z\, \sigma(z)
$$

Three weight matrices instead of two, so the hidden width is set to $$\tfrac{2}{3} \cdot 4d$$ to keep the parameter count of a standard $$4d$$ MLP.

**Rotary embeddings.** Absolute positional embeddings are removed and RoPE (Part 1) is applied to queries and keys in every layer.

Sizes (Table 2): 7B (32 layers, $$d = 4096$$, 32 heads), 13B (40, 5120, 40), 33B (60, 6656, 52), 65B (80, 8192, 64). AdamW with $$\beta_2 = 0.95$$, cosine schedule to 10%, weight decay 0.1, gradient clipping 1.0, 2000 warmup steps, 4M-token batches; the two small models see 1.0T tokens, the two large ones 1.4T.

## Efficient training (Section 2.4)

A causal multi-head attention implementation from xformers that does not store the masked scores or materialize the mask (the FlashAttention idea, Part 6); activation checkpointing that saves the expensive linear-layer outputs and recomputes the rest, implemented by writing the backward pass by hand; overlapping activation computation with communication across GPUs. The 65B model processes about 380 tokens/second/GPU on 2048 A100-80GB, so 1.4T tokens take about 21 days.

## Results

- **Common-sense reasoning (Table 3):** LLaMA-65B beats Chinchilla-70B on all but one benchmark and PaLM-540B on most; LLaMA-13B beats GPT-3 on most despite being 10× smaller.
- **Closed-book QA (Table 4):** state of the art on NaturalQuestions and TriviaQA for the 65B model; the 13B model is competitive with GPT-3 and Chinchilla and runs on one V100.
- **Reading comprehension (RACE), math (MATH, GSM8K) and code (HumanEval, MBPP):** LLaMA-65B is above PaLM-62B and close to PaLM-540B on GSM8K without any math-specific data, and beats PaLM and LaMDA on code at equal size.
- **MMLU (Table 9):** 63.4% 5-shot for the 65B, below Chinchilla-70B (67.5%) and PaLM-540B (69.3%); the authors attribute the gap to the small amount of books and academic text (177GB vs Gopher's 2TB).
- **Instruction fine-tuning (Section 4):** a short fine-tune ("LLaMA-I") reaches 68.9% MMLU, above Flan-PaLM-cont 62B.
- **Bias and toxicity (Section 5):** RealToxicityPrompts, CrowS-Pairs, WinoGender and TruthfulQA results are reported and are about on par with GPT-3 and OPT; toxicity increases with model size.

## Thoughts

- The paper's stated goal is best performance at a *fixed inference budget*, which is why it trains beyond compute-optimal; that framing reconciled Chinchilla with what practitioners actually deploy.
- Nothing in the architecture is new; the value is the choice of what to keep (RMSNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE) and what to drop (biases, learned positions, post-norm), plus public data.
- LLaMA-7B and 13B are the models QLoRA (next) and thousands of fine-tuning projects run on a single GPU.
