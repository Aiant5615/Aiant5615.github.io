## One-line summary

Apply the [DeepSeek-R1](/blog/deepseek-r1/) recipe to a multimodal LLM. Step one, try R1-Zero (GRPO with format + correctness rewards on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 10K math problems): the model does not develop long, self-reflective reasoning. Step two, build a **cold-start** dataset of 200K human-like multimodal chains of thought without human labels, by having an MLLM describe the image in enough detail that text-only DeepSeek-R1 can solve the problem, then pairing R1's thinking with the image. Step three, notice that the cold-started model **overthinks** (its correct answers come from its shorter chains), and run GRPO with **Progressive Thinking Suppression Training**: cap the response at 4K tokens with 16 samples per group, then 8K with 8, then 16K with 4. Vision-R1-7B reaches 73.5% on MathVista, 0.4 below OpenAI o1, and the 32B/72B versions reach 76.4%/78.2%.

## Why it matters

It is one of the first careful reports on what transfers from R1 to vision and what does not. The negative result (RL-only stalls in the multimodal setting given modest data) and the positive engineering (modality bridging for cold-start data, a length curriculum for RL) shaped most 2025 "R1 for VLMs" work.

## Can RL alone do it? (Section 3.1)

Vision-R1-Zero: GRPO on the base MLLM with the R1 system prompt (think inside `<think>` tags, answer inside `<answer>` tags) and two rewards in a 1:1 ratio, a formatting reward and a result reward (final answer matches ground truth). Result (Figure 1 D–F): the model does learn to write longer outputs over long training, but accuracy does not rise with length and it never produces the questioning/reflection behaviour that R1 shows. The paper's diagnosis: the base MLLM has too little exposure to complex multimodal CoT for outcome-only RL to find it, at least at 7B scale and 10K problems.

## Cold start by modality bridging (Section 3.3.1, Figure 2)

DeepSeek-R1 writes the kind of chain the authors want (with "wait, let me re-check…" style reflection) but is text-only. The pipeline:

1. Feed the image, question and answer to an MLLM to produce a **pseudo-CoT**: a caption plus a step-level reasoning sketch. This surfaces the visual details that the reasoning actually needs.
2. Feed the image, question *and* the pseudo-CoT back to the MLLM with a prompt asking for a description "containing all the necessary details of the image to answer the question". Descriptions produced this way are far richer than plain captions (Figure 5 in the appendix compares them).
3. Send description + question to DeepSeek-R1, keep chains whose final answer matches ground truth, apply rule-based filtering for logical inconsistency and wording, and post-process into `<think>…</think><answer>…</answer>`.
4. Attach the original image to the chain. The result is the 200K **Vision-R1-cold** dataset; Table 4 shows its reflective-marker frequency ("wait", "check", "alternatively") is far higher than in LLaVA-CoT or Mulberry data.

SFT on this set gives **Vision-R1-CI** (cold-start initialized).

## The overthinking optimization problem (Section 3.3.2)

Vision-R1-CI imitates R1's long deliberation, but on the 10K math set its *correct* responses cluster at shorter lengths (Figure 1A). If RL is started with a 16K token budget, the model lengthens its answers to "look like" complex reasoning without getting more accurate (Figure 1 D–E), and optimization stalls. The model must first learn to think *correctly*, then think *longer*.

## GRPO with PTST (Section 3.4, Figure 3)

GRPO is unchanged from [DeepSeekMath](/blog/deepseekmath-grpo/): for a question, sample a group of $$G$$ responses from the old policy, compute $$A_i = (r_i - \text{mean})/\text{std}$$ within the group, and maximize the clipped ratio objective with a KL term to the reference model. The reward is a **hard formatting result reward function (HFRRF)**: a response earns credit only if it is both correctly formatted *and* correct, so partial credit for format alone (which encourages length padding) is removed.

**Progressive Thinking Suppression Training** schedules the response-length limit and the group size across stages:

| Stage | Max thinking length | Group size $$G$$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 4K tokens | 16 |
| 2 | 8K tokens | 8 |
| 3 | 16K tokens | 4 |

Early on, the short cap forces the model to compress its reasoning to the essential, correct steps (suppressing the imitated verbosity); later the cap is relaxed so that genuinely hard problems can be worked through at length. The final released 7B model stops after stage 2 (the stage-3 box is dotted in Figure 3). Table 5 ablates the schedule: a fixed 16K×4 setting underperforms the progressive one, and Table 3 shows cold start, GRPO and PTST each contribute, with the average response length shrinking then growing as intended.

## Results (Section 4)

- **Math benchmarks (Table 1).** Vision-R1-7B: MathVista 73.5 (Qwen2.5-VL-7B 68.1, o1 73.9), MathVerse 52.4, MM-Math 40.2, DynaMath 56.3, an average gain of about 6 points over the base with only 10K RL problems. The 32B and 72B models (with more RL data) reach MathVista 76.4 and 78.2, about +10 average.
- **General benchmarks (Table 2).** Fine-tuning Llama-3.2-11B-Vision on Vision-R1-cold improves general multimodal benchmarks too, so the cold-start data is not math-specific.
- **Cold start matters (Table 6).** Zero+PTST (no cold start) and Zero+SFT-without-CoT+PTST both trail the full recipe; the reflective cold start is what RL amplifies.
- **Behaviour (Figure 4).** On MathVerse the 7B model shows "aha"-style re-examination of the figure mid-chain.

## Thoughts

- The cold-start construction is the clever part: rather than distilling a multimodal teacher (none existed at R1 quality), it converts the image into text carefully enough that a text reasoner can do the thinking, then puts the image back. It is a labelling pipeline, not a model.
- PTST is a length curriculum with a clear rationale, and the "correct answers are the short ones" diagnostic is a useful thing to check in any R1-style project before turning up the token budget.
- The reasoning, however, is still all in text: the image is read once at the start. The next paper makes the model look again during the chain.
