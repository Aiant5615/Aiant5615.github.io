## One-line summary

Learns control policies for Atari games end-to-end from raw pixels by combining a CNN-based Q-function with experience replay and Q-learning.

## Problem

Learning control directly from high-dimensional sensory input has long been a hard problem in reinforcement learning. Deep learning had succeeded in vision, but RL differs from supervised learning in three ways: rewards are sparse and delayed, consecutive samples are strongly correlated, and the data distribution shifts as the policy changes.

## Key ideas

- **Q-network**: a CNN takes the state (a stack of the last 4 frames) and outputs Q-values for every action at once.
- **Experience replay**: transitions $(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})$ are stored in a buffer and minibatches are sampled uniformly at random. This breaks sample correlation and improves data efficiency.
- **Loss**:

$$
L_i(\theta_i) = \mathbb{E}_{(s,a,r,s') \sim \mathcal{D}} \left[ \left( r + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s', a'; \theta_{i-1}) - Q(s, a; \theta_i) \right)^2 \right]
$$

- Rewards are clipped to $[-1, 1]$ so that one learning rate works across games with very different score scales.

## Results

Outperformed prior methods on six of seven Atari games and beat a human expert on three. The important part is that the same network and hyperparameters were used for every game, with no per-game tuning.

## Thoughts

- The Nature 2015 follow-up added a **target network**, which greatly improved stability. The loss above already uses $\theta_{i-1}$ as the target, so the seed of that idea is here.
- Implementing DQN in Lab 4 made it clear how sensitive the learning curve is to the replay buffer size and the $\epsilon$ schedule. Now I understand why the paper leaves so many hyperparameters unstated.
