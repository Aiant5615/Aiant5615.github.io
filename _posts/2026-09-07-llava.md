## One-line summary

Generate multimodal instruction-following data by giving GPT-4 (text-only) the captions and object boxes of an image and asking for conversations, descriptions and reasoning questions; then connect a frozen CLIP image encoder to an open LLM (Vicuna) with a single linear layer and fine-tune on that data. The result talks about images like a chat assistant and reaches 85% of GPT-4's score on a new multimodal benchmark, plus a state of the art on ScienceQA.

## Why it matters

[BLIP-2](/blog/blip2/) still needed careful pretraining objectives. LLaVA shows that with *instruction* data, the connector can be trivial and the LLM can be fine-tuned end to end, which made "vision encoder + projector + LLM" the standard open recipe. Its GPT-4-generated dataset also started the practice of synthesizing multimodal training data with a stronger text model.

## Architecture (Figure 1)

For an image $$X_v$$, the frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 encoder $$g$$ gives grid features $$Z_v = g(X_v)$$ (the paper uses the features before the last Transformer layer). A trainable projection matrix $$W$$ maps them into the LLM's word-embedding space:

$$
H_v = W \cdot Z_v, \qquad Z_v = g(X_v)
$$

so each image becomes a sequence of visual tokens $$H_v$$ with the same dimensionality as text embeddings. Language instructions $$X_q$$ are embedded as usual ($$H_q$$), and the LLM $$f_{\phi}$$ (Vicuna, 13B) generates the answer $$X_a$$. The paper notes this is deliberately the simplest possible connector and that Flamingo-style cross-attention or a Q-Former could replace it.

## The data (Section 3)

COCO images come with captions and bounding boxes. These *symbolic* descriptions are given to text-only GPT-4 together with a few hand-written seed examples, and GPT-4 is prompted to produce three kinds of data, 158K samples in total:

- **Conversation (58K):** multi-turn Q&A about the visible content, as if the assistant could see the image.
- **Detailed description (23K):** a long description in response to a randomly sampled prompt like "Describe the following image in detail".
- **Complex reasoning (77K):** questions needing reasoning beyond recognition ("what challenges might these people face?"), with step-by-step answers.

The image is never shown to GPT-4; the boxes give it spatial information. The authors found GPT-4 produced better data than ChatGPT, especially for spatial reasoning.

## Training

Each image's conversation $$(X_q^1, X_a^1, \dots, X_q^T, X_a^T)$$ is turned into one sequence where at the first turn the image tokens are placed before or after the question at random and every turn follows the Vicuna chat format. The model is trained with the ordinary autoregressive loss, computed only on the assistant's answer tokens:

$$
p(X_a \mid X_v, X_{\text{instruct}}) = \prod_{i=1}^{L} p_{\theta}\big(x_i \mid X_v,\ X_{\text{instruct}, <i},\ X_{a, <i}\big)
$$

Two stages:

1. **Feature alignment.** 595K image–caption pairs filtered from CC3M, formatted as a trivial instruction ("describe the image briefly") with the caption as the answer. Only $$W$$ is trained; the encoder and LLM are frozen. This teaches the projection to speak the LLM's language.
2. **End-to-end fine-tuning.** On the 158K instruction data (and, for the ScienceQA experiment, on that dataset); the encoder stays frozen, $$W$$ and the LLM are trained. About a day on 8 A100s for the 13B model.

## Evaluation

- **LLaVA-Bench (COCO and In-the-Wild).** GPT-4 (text-only, given the same symbolic image description) writes reference answers; GPT-4 then scores the candidate answer against the reference on a 1–10 scale. LLaVA reaches **85.1%** of GPT-4's score overall; ablations show conversation-only data gives most of the gain and detailed/reasoning data adds 7 points.
- **ScienceQA.** Fine-tuned LLaVA gets 90.92%; combining it with GPT-4 as a judge ("GPT-4 decides when the two disagree") reaches **92.53%**, the state of the art at the time.
- Qualitative: it explains the "extreme ironing" meme, and reproduces the GPT-4 technical report's website-from-a-sketch example (Figure 2) and multi-turn visual dialogue (Figure 3), which no open model could do before.

## Thoughts

- The linear projection works because the LLM is fine-tuned; freeze the LLM and you need a Q-Former. The follow-up ([LLaVA-1.5](/blog/llava-15/)) shows even a two-layer MLP helps.
- Evaluating with GPT-4 as judge, on references GPT-4 also wrote, is circular; the paper acknowledges it and adds ScienceQA as a hard number. The community later built proper benchmarks (MMBench, MM-Vet, POPE).
- Synthetic instruction data from a text-only model is the cheap trick that unlocked open multimodal assistants; its limits (hallucinated details GPT-4 cannot see) are what later data work fixed.
