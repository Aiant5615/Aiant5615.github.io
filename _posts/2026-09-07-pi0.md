## One-line summary

Keep the VLM (PaliGemma, 3B) for perception and language, but give actions their own **action expert**, a smaller transformer that attends to the VLM's tokens and generates a chunk of 50 continuous actions with **flow matching** instead of predicting tokens. Pre-train on about 10,000 hours of demonstrations across 7 robot configurations and 68 tasks, then post-train on curated data for specific tasks. The model folds laundry from a dryer, bags groceries, buses tables and assembles boxes, and beats OpenVLA, Octo, ACT and Diffusion Policy on out-of-the-box and fine-tuned evaluations.

## Why it matters

RT-2 and OpenVLA showed VLM knowledge transfers to control but were stuck at discrete actions and a few Hz. Octo showed diffusion heads give precise continuous actions but had no VLM. π0 combines them: VLM backbone, continuous generative action head, high-frequency chunks, and dexterous bimanual and mobile tasks, which is the design most current VLAs follow.

## Architecture (Figure 1, Figure 3)

**VLM backbone.** PaliGemma (SigLIP vision encoder + Gemma 2B LM) processes the images (up to 3 cameras) and the language instruction. Its weights are initialized from the public checkpoint so web-scale semantics come for free.

**Action expert.** A second set of transformer weights (about 300M parameters) handles the robot-specific tokens: the proprioceptive state $$q_t$$ and the noisy action chunk $$A_t^{\tau}$$. The two experts share the same layers' attention but have separate weights, a mixture-of-experts-like split by token type. Attention is **block-wise causal** in three blocks: image and language tokens; the state token; the action tokens. Image/language tokens cannot attend to robot tokens (so the VLM behaves as pretrained), state attends to the first block, actions attend to everything, and within the action block attention is bidirectional.

**Action chunking.** The model outputs $$A_t = [a_t, a_{t+1}, \dots, a_{t+H-1}]$$ with $$H = 50$$, so at 50 Hz a single forward pass covers one second of motion. Different robots have different action dimensions; all are padded to the largest (18 for two arms plus a mobile base).

## Flow matching for actions (Section IV)

Instead of predicting discrete bins, the action expert learns a vector field that transports Gaussian noise to the data distribution of action chunks, conditioned on the observation $$o_t$$. For a flow time $$\tau \in [0, 1]$$, form a noisy chunk by linear interpolation between noise and the true chunk,

$$
A_t^{\tau} = \tau A_t + (1 - \tau)\,\epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)
$$

whose "velocity" toward the data is $$u(A_t^{\tau} \mid A_t) = \epsilon - A_t$$. The expert $$v_{\theta}$$ is trained to predict that velocity,

$$
L^{\tau}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{p(A_t \mid o_t),\ q(A_t^{\tau} \mid A_t)}\Big[\big\lVert v_{\theta}(A_t^{\tau}, o_t) - u(A_t^{\tau} \mid A_t)\big\rVert^2\Big]
$$

This is the conditional flow-matching objective (the linear-Gaussian probability path), a close relative of the diffusion loss in Octo but with a straight interpolation and no noise schedule to tune. π0 samples $$\tau$$ from a **beta distribution that emphasizes low $$\tau$$** (noisier samples), on the argument that for action prediction the coarse structure decided at high noise levels matters most.

**Inference.** Start from $$A_t^{0} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$$ and integrate the learned field with forward Euler steps,

$$
A_t^{\tau + \delta} = A_t^{\tau} + \delta\, v_{\theta}(A_t^{\tau}, o_t), \qquad \delta = 0.1
$$

i.e. **10 steps**, each a forward pass of the action expert only (the VLM's keys and values are cached), which is what makes 50 Hz feasible on an onboard GPU.

## Data and training recipe (Figure 3, Figure 4)

- **Pre-training mixture:** 903M timesteps; 9.1% from Open X-Embodiment and the rest from Physical Intelligence's own 7 robot configurations (single-arm UR5e, bimanual UR5e, Franka, bimanual Trossen, bimanual ARX, mobile Trossen, mobile Fibocom) over 68 tasks, weighted by $$n^{0.43}$$ per task-robot combination so large datasets do not dominate. The pre-training data is deliberately broad and includes recoveries and mistakes.
- **Post-training:** a smaller, high-quality curated set for the target tasks (5–100+ hours each), which teaches consistent, efficient strategies. The pre-train/post-train split mirrors LLM pretraining followed by instruction tuning.
- A **π0-small** (470M) variant without the VLM is trained for ablations; a language-following experiment uses a high-level VLM planner that issues sub-task instructions to π0.

## Results (Section V)

- **Out-of-the-box (Figure 6, Section V.A):** after pre-training only, on shirt folding, bussing (easy/hard), grocery bagging and toast, π0 achieves the highest average score by a wide margin; OpenVLA and Octo (fine-tuned to the same data) are near zero on most, and the π0-small ablation shows the VLM backbone is what carries the difficult tasks.
- **Language following (V.B):** with a high-level VLM emitting sub-instructions, π0 completes long tasks (bussing, table setting, grocery bagging) more often than with a flat instruction; π0-small benefits far less from language.
- **Fine-tuning to new dexterous tasks (V.C):** on tasks like stacking bowls, folding towels, microwave, paper-towel replacement, fine-tuning π0 beats OpenVLA, ACT and Diffusion Policy trained from scratch, and beats training π0 from scratch, especially in the low-data regime.
- **Complex multi-stage tasks (V.D):** laundry folding from a bin (mobile and static), dryer unloading, table bussing with novel objects, box building, to-go box packing and egg boxing, scored by rubric; π0 with pre-training then post-training scores highest on all, and pre-training matters most on the hardest tasks (laundry).

## Thoughts

- The two-expert design is the key move: the VLM stays a VLM, and a separate, small, fast network does control. It resolves the RT-2 speed problem without giving up the pretrained semantics.
- Flow matching vs diffusion is a minor difference; *continuous chunked actions from a generative head* is the substantive change from RT-2/OpenVLA, and 50 Hz is what makes laundry folding possible.
- The recipe's shape (broad pre-training, curated post-training, language planning on top) is the LLM recipe transplanted, and the series ends where the LLM-RL series began: with a base model that needs the right post-training.
