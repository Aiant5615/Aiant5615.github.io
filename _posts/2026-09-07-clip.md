## One-line summary

Train an image encoder and a text encoder jointly so that the embeddings of a matching (image, caption) pair are close and non-matching pairs are far, on 400 million pairs scraped from the web. The result is a vision model that classifies images *zero-shot* by comparing them with text prompts like "a photo of a dog", matching a supervised ResNet-50 on ImageNet without seeing a single ImageNet label.

## Why it matters

CLIP's image encoder is the eye of LLaVA and BLIP-2 (a ViT-L/14 or ViT-g trained this way), and CLIP's insight that *language is a supervision signal for vision* is what makes multimodal LLMs possible at all. Its zero-shot prompting is also the visual counterpart of GPT-3's few-shot prompting, published a year later.

## The objective

Given a batch of $$N$$ pairs, compute image embeddings $$I_e \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_e}$$ and text embeddings $$T_e \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d_e}$$ (each encoder's output projected linearly into a shared space and $$\ell_2$$-normalized), then the $$N \times N$$ matrix of scaled cosine similarities

$$
\text{logits} = \big(I_e T_e^{\top}\big) \cdot e^{t}
$$

with a learned temperature $$t$$ (initialized to $$\log(1/0.07)$$ and clipped so the scale never exceeds 100). The training signal is a symmetric cross-entropy: the $$i$$-th image should pick the $$i$$-th text among the $$N$$ texts, and the $$i$$-th text should pick the $$i$$-th image among the $$N$$ images,

$$
\mathcal{L} = \tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\operatorname{CE}_{\text{rows}}(\text{logits}, [1..N]) + \operatorname{CE}_{\text{cols}}(\text{logits}, [1..N])\Big)
$$

There are $$N$$ positive pairs and $$N^2 - N$$ negatives per batch, which is why the batch is huge (32,768). This is the InfoNCE / multi-class N-pair loss; the paper's contribution is applying it at web scale with natural-language captions rather than class labels. Figure 3 is the whole thing in a dozen lines of NumPy-style pseudocode; the two `cross_entropy_loss` calls with `axis=0` and `axis=1` are the two halves of the loss.

**Why contrastive, not generative.** Section 2.3 and Figure 2: predicting the exact caption words (a transformer language model on top of the image) is 3× less data-efficient than a bag-of-words objective, which is itself 4× less efficient than the contrastive objective, measured by zero-shot ImageNet accuracy vs images processed. Captions are noisy and the exact wording is unpredictable; which caption *matches* is learnable.

## Encoders and data

Image encoders: modified ResNet-50/101 and scaled versions (RN50x4, x16, x64, EfficientNet-style scaling), and ViT-B/32, B/16, L/14 (plus L/14 at 336 px, the best model). Text encoder: a 12-layer, 512-wide, 8-head Transformer over lower-cased byte-pair-encoded text (49,152 vocabulary, 76 tokens max), with the `[EOS]` token's final activation as the text embedding. Data: **WIT**, 400M (image, text) pairs collected by searching for 500k query strings and balancing to 20k pairs per query. Training: 32 epochs, Adam with decoupled weight decay, cosine schedule, mixed precision; the largest ResNet took 18 days on 592 V100s, ViT-L/14 12 days on 256 V100s.

## Zero-shot transfer

Turn any classification dataset into a retrieval task: embed each class name inside a template ("A photo of a {label}."), embed the image, and predict the class whose text embedding is closest. Section 3.1.4: the template matters, since a bare word is ambiguous ("crane", "boxer") and captions are sentences; dataset-specific templates ("a satellite photo of a {label}", "a photo of a {label}, a type of pet") and **ensembling** 80 templates by averaging their embeddings add 3.5 points on ImageNet (Figure 4).

## Results

- **Zero-shot ImageNet:** 76.2% top-1 for ViT-L/14@336, matching the original supervised ResNet-50, with no ImageNet training data.
- **Figure 5 (zero-shot vs a fully supervised linear probe on ResNet-50 features)** across 27 datasets: CLIP wins on 16, by huge margins on StanfordCars (+28.9) and Kinetics700, and loses on specialized or abstract tasks (EuroSAT satellite images, KITTI distance, tumor detection, counting).
- **Figure 6 (zero-shot vs few-shot):** zero-shot CLIP matches a 4-shot linear probe on its own features and a 16-shot probe on the best previous models (BiT-M, SimCLRv2); more labelled examples still help.
- **Robustness (Section 3.3):** on ImageNet distribution shifts (Sketch, -R, -A, ObjectNet) zero-shot CLIP loses far less accuracy than models trained on ImageNet, and adapting it to ImageNet *reduces* that robustness, suggesting the robustness comes from not overfitting to one label set.
- **Linear probes (Section 3.2):** CLIP features are the best available for 21 of 27 datasets.

## Thoughts

- A frozen CLIP image tower plus a text-side model is the template for every "connect a vision encoder to an LLM" paper that follows. What changes is the connector.
- The contrastive objective learns *global* alignment (one vector per image); fine spatial detail, counting and reading text are exactly what CLIP is bad at (Figure 5's losers) and what later models add via higher resolution and more tokens.
- Prompt ensembling for images and few-shot prompting for text arrived within a year of each other; both say the same thing: task specification can be text.
