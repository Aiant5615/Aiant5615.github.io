## One-line summary

[DDPO](/blog/ddpo/) needs a stochastic per-step policy; a flow-matching model samples by integrating a deterministic ODE, so there is nothing to take a log-probability of. Flow-GRPO rewrites the rectified-flow ODE as an SDE that has exactly the same marginal distribution at every time, discretizes it with Euler–Maruyama (each step becomes an isotropic Gaussian), and then applies [GRPO](/blog/deepseekmath-grpo/): sample a group of images per prompt, normalize rewards within the group, clip the per-step likelihood ratio, add a closed-form KL to the reference. Because RL rollouts do not need to be pretty, training samples with $$T = 10$$ steps while inference keeps $$T = 40$$, a 4× speedup. On Stable Diffusion 3.5 Medium, GenEval accuracy goes 63% → 95% (above GPT-4o), text rendering 59% → 92%, and PickScore rises, with image quality on DrawBench essentially unchanged when the KL term is on.

## Why it matters

By 2025 the strongest open image and video generators were flow-matching transformers, not DDPMs, and RL for them meant offline preference methods (Flow-DPO). This paper is the bridge that lets the LLM reasoning-RL toolkit (GRPO, verifiable rewards, KL to reference) be used online on flow models, and its two tricks (ODE→SDE, denoising reduction) became the default in the follow-ups, including the one it is compared against next, DiffusionNFT.

## Preliminaries

**Rectified flow.** With data $$x_0$$ and noise $$x_1$$, define $$x_t = (1 - t)x_0 + t x_1$$ for $$t \in [0, 1]$$ and train $$v_{\theta}(x_t, t)$$ to regress the constant velocity $$v = x_1 - x_0$$:

$$
L(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t,\, x_0,\, x_1}\big[\lVert v - v_{\theta}(x_t, t)\rVert^2\big]
$$

Sampling integrates $$dx_t = v_t\, dt$$ from $$t = 1$$ (noise) to $$t = 0$$ (data), usually with Euler steps, a one-to-one map from noise to image. (Note the paper's convention: $$t = 1$$ is noise, the opposite of the DDPO review's indexing.)

**Denoising as an MDP.** Same as DDPO: state $$(c, t, x_t)$$, action the next $$x$$, policy $$p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c)$$, reward $$r(x_0, c)$$ only at the end.

## GRPO on flow matching (Section 4)

The regularized RL objective is

$$
\max_{\theta}\ \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_{\theta}}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{T}\big(R(s_t, a_t) - \beta\, D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}(\cdot \mid s_t)\,\Vert\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(\cdot \mid s_t))\big)\Big]
$$

For a prompt $$c$$, sample a group of $$G$$ images $$\{x_0^i\}$$ with their full reverse trajectories, and normalize the rewards within the group:

$$
\hat{A}_t^{i} = \frac{R(x_0^i, c) - \text{mean}\{R(x_0^j, c)\}_{j=1}^{G}}{\text{std}\{R(x_0^j, c)\}_{j=1}^{G}}
$$

The same advantage is assigned to every denoising step of trajectory $$i$$ (no per-step credit, no critic). The objective is then the PPO-style clipped surrogate averaged over the group and the timesteps:

$$
J_{\text{Flow-GRPO}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\Big[\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i=1}^{G}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\Big(\min\big(r_t^i(\theta)\hat{A}_t^i,\ \text{clip}(r_t^i(\theta), 1-\varepsilon, 1+\varepsilon)\hat{A}_t^i\big) - \beta\, D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}\Vert\pi_{\text{ref}})\Big)\Big]
$$

with the per-step ratio $$r_t^i(\theta) = p_{\theta}(x_{t-1}^i \mid x_t^i, c)\,/\,p_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(x_{t-1}^i \mid x_t^i, c)$$. Everything in this formula requires a *density* for each step, which is the problem the next section solves.

## From ODE to SDE (the key idea)

A deterministic sampler fails GRPO twice: the ratio needs $$p(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c)$$, which under an ODE would require divergence estimation, and there is no exploration beyond the initial seed. The fix is a reverse-time SDE that keeps the ODE's marginals $$p_t(x_t)$$ for all $$t$$ (the standard result relating probability-flow ODEs and SDEs):

$$
dx_t = \Big(v_t(x_t) - \frac{\sigma_t^2}{2}\nabla \log p_t(x_t)\Big)dt + \sigma_t\, dw
$$

where $$dw$$ is a Wiener increment and $$\sigma_t$$ sets how much noise is injected. For rectified flow the score can be written in terms of the velocity, giving

$$
dx_t = \Big(v_t(x_t) + \frac{\sigma_t^2}{2t}\big(x_t + (1 - t)\,v_t(x_t)\big)\Big)dt + \sigma_t\, dw
$$

Euler–Maruyama discretization then gives the update actually implemented:

$$
x_{t+\Delta t} = x_t + \Big(v_{\theta}(x_t, t) + \frac{\sigma_t^2}{2t}\big(x_t + (1-t)v_{\theta}(x_t, t)\big)\Big)\Delta t + \sigma_t\sqrt{\Delta t}\ \epsilon, \qquad \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)
$$

The paper uses $$\sigma_t = a\sqrt{t/(1-t)}$$ with a scalar noise level $$a$$ (0.7 is best for OCR; too little noise slows learning, too much hurts quality). Two consequences:

1. The policy $$\pi_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c)$$ is an isotropic Gaussian with mean given by the bracket and variance $$\sigma_t^2 \Delta t$$, so the ratio $$r_t^i$$ is exact, just as in DDPO.
2. The KL between the current and reference policies is a difference of Gaussian means and has the closed form

$$
D_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\theta}\Vert\pi_{\text{ref}}) = \frac{\lVert x_{t+\Delta t, \theta} - x_{t+\Delta t, \text{ref}}\rVert^2}{2\sigma_t^2 \Delta t} = \frac{\Delta t}{2}\Big(\frac{\sigma_t(1-t)}{2t} + \frac{1}{\sigma_t}\Big)^2 \lVert v_{\theta}(x_t, t) - v_{\text{ref}}(x_t, t)\rVert^2
$$

i.e. a weighted squared distance between the two velocity fields at the same point, cheap to compute alongside the loss.

## Denoising reduction

Online RL is bottlenecked by sampling. The observation is that the *rollouts used for training* can be low quality: collect trajectories with $$T = 10$$ SDE steps while keeping the model's default $$T = 40$$ at inference. Reducing 40 → 10 gives over 4× faster convergence on all three tasks with no loss in final reward (Figure 7a); going to 5 stops helping.

## Tasks and rewards (Section 5.1)

- **Compositional generation (GenEval).** Prompts built from GenEval templates (counting, position, attribute binding, colors, two objects), test set deduplicated against order-swapped prompts. Rewards are rule-based: counting $$r = 1 - \lvert N_{\text{gen}} - N_{\text{ref}}\rvert / N_{\text{ref}}$$; for position/color, partial credit for the right count and the rest for the right relation or color, all checked with an object detector.
- **Visual text rendering.** Prompts "A sign that says 'text'"; reward $$r = \max(1 - N_e / N_{\text{ref}}, 0)$$ with $$N_e$$ the edit distance between OCR output and target.
- **Human preference.** PickScore as the reward model.
- **Reward-hacking probes.** Aesthetic score, DeQA, ImageReward and UnifiedReward on DrawBench, a prompt set unrelated to the training prompts.

## Results

- **GenEval (Table 1, Figure 3).** SD3.5-M overall 0.63 → 0.95, with Position 0.24 → 0.99 and Counting 0.50 → 0.95; above GPT-4o (0.84) and SANA-1.5 4.8B (0.81). The qualitative grid shows correct counts of giraffes, a purple broccoli, and a bench to the left of a bear.
- **Text and preference (Table 2).** OCR accuracy 0.59 → 0.92; PickScore 21.7 → 23.3. With KL on, the DrawBench quality metrics stay at or above the base model; with KL off they drop (Aesthetic 5.39 → 4.93, DeQA 4.07 → 2.77 on the GenEval run).
- **vs other alignment methods (Figure 4).** Same group sampling, different update: online/offline SFT on the best image, online/offline Flow-DPO on best-vs-worst pairs. Flow-GRPO is clearly ahead; online DPO beats offline DPO and is sensitive to $$\beta$$.
- **Group size (Figure 5).** $$G = 24$$ beats 12 beats 6 on PickScore; small groups eventually collapse.
- **Reward hacking (Figure 6, Section 5.3).** Without KL, GenEval/OCR runs lose visual quality and PickScore runs lose diversity (every seed converges to one style). KL is not the same as early stopping: with it, the same peak reward is reached, more slowly, without the collapse.
- **Generalization (Table 4).** Trained on 60 object classes, works on 20 unseen ones; trained to count 2–4 objects, extrapolates to 5–6.

## Thoughts

- The ODE→SDE step is textbook stochastic calculus, but choosing to *use* it for exploration and likelihoods is what unlocks the whole GRPO machinery for flow models. Once each step is Gaussian, the derivation is DDPO-IS with a group baseline.
- Denoising reduction is the most practical idea in the paper: rollouts are for gradient signal, not for looking at. It halves the cost problem that made online RL on big generators look impractical.
- Two things remain awkward, and the next paper attacks both: training is tied to a first-order SDE sampler (no ODE or high-order solvers for rollouts), and the recipe still relies on classifier-free guidance at inference, which the RL never sees.
