## One-line summary

Train a single transformer on 800,000 robot trajectories from 25 datasets and 9 robot types, with a design that makes the model an *adaptable* starting point: any input becomes tokens, any output is read from "readout" tokens, and the attention mask is block-wise so that new observation or action modalities can be attached after pretraining. Out of the box it controls robots at four institutions; with about 100 demonstrations and a few hours on one GPU it adapts to new robots, sensors and action spaces, beating policies trained from scratch and prior generalist models.

## Why it matters

RT-1 and RT-2 were closed and tied to one robot embodiment. Octo is the first open generalist policy with weights, code and a clean fine-tuning story, and its **diffusion action head** replaced discrete action tokens as the way to output precise continuous actions, a line continued by π0's flow matching.

## Architecture (Figure 2)

**Tokenizers.** Language instructions are embedded by a pretrained T5-base encoder; goal images and observation images (third-person and wrist cameras) are split into patches and embedded by a shallow convolutional stem; proprioception can be added as extra tokens. Every input is a token sequence, so adding a modality means adding a tokenizer.

**Transformer backbone.** A standard transformer (Octo-Small: 27M parameters, 12 layers; Octo-Base: 93M, 12 layers, larger width) over task tokens and a window of observation tokens. **Readout tokens** are appended to each timestep; they attend to task and observation tokens but nothing attends to them, so they act as a learned summary (like `[CLS]`) that heads read from. Attention is **block-wise causal**: observation tokens at time $$t$$ attend to task tokens and observations at $$\le t$$; masks are also used to drop out absent modalities (e.g. no wrist camera) so one model handles heterogeneous datasets, and to let new tokens be added at fine-tuning time without changing existing weights.

**Diffusion action head.** Rather than classifying bins, the head generates a *chunk* of future actions (e.g. 4 steps) with a conditional diffusion process. Given the readout embedding $$e$$, a small MLP $$\epsilon_{\theta}(x^k, e, k)$$ is trained to predict the noise added to the action chunk $$a$$ at diffusion step $$k$$,

$$
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{a,\ k,\ \epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)}\Big[\big\lVert \epsilon - \epsilon_{\theta}\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_k}\, a + \sqrt{1 - \bar\alpha_k}\, \epsilon,\ e,\ k\big)\big\rVert^2\Big]
$$

and at inference a sample is drawn by starting from Gaussian noise and iterating the DDPM update $$x^{k-1} = \alpha\big(x^k - \gamma\, \epsilon_{\theta}(x^k, e, k)\big) + \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2 I)$$ for 20 steps. Diffusion heads capture multi-modal action distributions (several valid ways to grasp) that a mean-regression or a per-dimension classifier smears out; the paper's ablation (Table 2) shows the diffusion head beats MSE and discrete heads.

## Data and training (Figure 3)

A curated mixture of 25 Open X-Embodiment datasets, 800k trajectories, reweighted so that no single dataset dominates and datasets with more diversity get more weight (Bridge, RT-1 data and others make up the largest slices). Training on TPU v4 for 300k steps with batch 2048, augmentations on images, random dropout of goal images and instructions so the model works with either conditioning, and action chunk prediction.

## Fine-tuning recipe (Section 4)

Attach new tokenizers/heads as needed (a new camera, force-torque sensor, a 14-D bimanual action space), keep the pretrained transformer, and train everything for about 50k steps with a small learning rate, on ~100 target demonstrations, in under 5 hours on a consumer GPU (or a single A5000). The block-wise mask makes new tokens simply additional columns.

## Results (Section 5)

- **Zero-shot (Figure 5):** across 9 real setups at 4 institutions (WidowX, UR5, Franka, and others; Figure 4), Octo-Base succeeds out of the box on tasks in the pretraining distribution and beats RT-1-X (the OXE-trained RT-1) by about 30 points on average and RT-2-X (55B) on the WidowX and UR5 tasks it was compared on.
- **Fine-tuning (Table 1):** on six new setups including a new action space (bimanual ALOHA) and new inputs (force-torque for a Berkeley insertion task), fine-tuned Octo beats training from scratch by a large margin and also beats fine-tuned VC-1 (a visual pretrained encoder) on average, at a fraction of the compute of VLA-scale models.
- **Design ablations (Table 2, Figure 6):** diffusion head > MSE > discrete; a ResNet-style stem underperforms the patch tokenizer at scale; more OXE data and larger models help (Octo-Base > Octo-Small on both UR5 and WidowX).
- **Limitations:** wrist cameras are underused, language conditioning is weaker than goal-image conditioning, and the largest model is still small (93M); the authors expect scaling to help.

## Thoughts

- Octo separates two things RT-2 bundled: *representation* (a transformer over tokens) and *action decoding* (diffusion). The second is what later VLAs took from it.
- Its backbone is tiny because it does not start from a VLM; OpenVLA does the opposite (7B VLM, discrete actions). π0 combines both: a VLM backbone with a flow-matching action expert.
- The fine-tuning flexibility (new sensors, new action spaces) is a design goal no VLM-based VLA fully matched, and is why Octo stayed a common baseline.
