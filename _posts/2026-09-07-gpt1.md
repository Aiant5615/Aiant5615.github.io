## One-line summary

Train a 12-layer Transformer *decoder* to predict the next token on 7,000 unpublished books, then, for each downstream task, reformat the task's input as a single token sequence and fine-tune the whole network with a small linear head, keeping the language-model loss as a regularizer. Nine of twelve benchmarks improve on the state of the art.

## Why it matters

This is the "GPT" in every later GPT. It establishes the two-stage recipe (unsupervised pretraining, supervised fine-tuning), the choice of a *causal* decoder-only Transformer, and the trick of expressing every task as text with delimiter tokens rather than designing task-specific architectures. [BERT](/blog/bert/), four months later, keeps the recipe and changes the direction of attention.

## Stage 1: unsupervised pretraining

Given an unlabeled token stream $$\mathcal{U} = (u_1, \dots, u_n)$$, maximize the standard left-to-right language-model likelihood with a context window of $$k$$ tokens,

$$
L_1(\mathcal{U}) = \sum_i \log P\big(u_i \mid u_{i-k}, \dots, u_{i-1};\ \Theta\big)
$$

The model is a multi-layer Transformer decoder (masked self-attention, then position-wise FFN, as in the [Transformer](/blog/transformer/) decoder without the cross-attention):

$$
h_0 = U W_e + W_p, \qquad h_l = \operatorname{transformer\_block}(h_{l-1}) \ \ \text{for } l = 1..n, \qquad P(u) = \operatorname{softmax}\big(h_n W_e^{\top}\big)
$$

$$U$$ is the matrix of context tokens (one-hot rows), $$W_e$$ the token embedding, $$W_p$$ a *learned* position embedding (not sinusoidal), and the output softmax reuses $$W_e$$ (tied embeddings). Configuration: 12 blocks, $$d_{\text{model}} = 768$$, 12 heads, FFN width 3072, GELU activations, 40k BPE merges, context 512, Adam with a cosine schedule peaking at $$2.5 \times 10^{-4}$$, 100 epochs of batch 64 on BooksCorpus (long contiguous text, chosen so the model learns long-range structure).

## Stage 2: supervised fine-tuning

For a labeled dataset $$\mathcal{C}$$ of token sequences $$x^1, \dots, x^m$$ with label $$y$$, run the pretrained model, take the final block's activation at the *last* token, $$h_l^m$$, and add one linear layer $$W_y$$:

$$
P(y \mid x^1, \dots, x^m) = \operatorname{softmax}\big(h_l^m W_y\big), \qquad L_2(\mathcal{C}) = \sum_{(x, y)} \log P(y \mid x^1, \dots, x^m)
$$

Because attention is causal, the last token is the only position that has seen the whole input, so it is the natural place to read out a decision. Fine-tuning optimizes a weighted sum that keeps the LM loss on the task text,

$$
L_3(\mathcal{C}) = L_2(\mathcal{C}) + \lambda\, L_1(\mathcal{C})
$$

with $$\lambda = 0.5$$. The auxiliary term improves generalization on large datasets and speeds convergence (Table 5 ablates it: removing it costs about 1–2 points on the bigger datasets). Only $$W_y$$ and the delimiter embeddings are new; everything else is transferred. Fine-tuning takes about 3 epochs at learning rate $$6.25 \times 10^{-5}$$, batch 32.

## Task-specific input transformations

The key design decision, shown in the right half of Figure 1: never change the architecture, change the *input string*.

- **Classification:** `[start] text [extract]` → linear head on the `[extract]` position.
- **Entailment:** `[start] premise [delim] hypothesis [extract]`.
- **Similarity:** the order of the two sentences should not matter, so run *both* orders `[start] A [delim] B [extract]` and `[start] B [delim] A [extract]`, add the two final vectors element-wise, then apply the head.
- **Multiple choice / QA:** for each candidate answer build `[start] context ; question [delim] answer_k [extract]`, score each with the head, and softmax across candidates.

Delimiter tokens are randomly initialized and learned during fine-tuning. This "textualize the task" idea is the ancestor of T5's prefixes and of prompting.

## Results

New state of the art on 9 of 12 tasks: natural language inference (MNLI 82.1, SciTail 88.3, QNLI 88.1, SNLI 89.9), question answering and commonsense (RACE 59.0, Story Cloze 86.5, +8.9 absolute), semantic similarity (STS-B 82.0, QQP 70.3), and a GLUE score of 72.8 vs 68.9 before. Ablations (Table 5): removing pretraining loses 14.8% on average; an LSTM in place of the Transformer loses 5.6%.

## Thoughts

- Reading the label from the last token is a consequence of causal masking; BERT's `[CLS]` at the *front* works because its attention is bidirectional. Same idea, opposite ends.
- The zero-shot experiments are the interesting part in hindsight. The paper treats them as an analysis; the next two OpenAI papers treat them as the goal.
- Practically, this paper made "download pretrained weights, fine-tune" the default NLP workflow.
