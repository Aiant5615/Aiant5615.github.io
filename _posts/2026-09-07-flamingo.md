## One-line summary

Keep a pretrained vision encoder and a pretrained language model frozen, insert new cross-attention layers into the LM that read a fixed number of visual tokens per image, train only the new parts on web pages with interleaved images and text, and you get a model that does captioning, VQA and visual dialogue from a handful of in-context examples, beating models fine-tuned on thousands of examples on several benchmarks.

## Why it matters

Flamingo is the first *few-shot* visual language model in the GPT-3 sense, and it fixes the design pattern later papers keep: frozen unimodal backbones, a small trainable bridge, and interleaved image–text sequences as input. BLIP-2 and LLaVA are cheaper variants of this bridge; Qwen2-VL trains everything but keeps the interleaving.

## Architecture (Figure 3)

**Vision encoder.** A NFNet-F6 pretrained contrastively (CLIP-style) on image–text pairs, then frozen. It produces a 2-D grid of features per image (or per video frame).

**Perceiver Resampler (Figure 5).** The variable-size grid (and variable number of frames) is mapped to a *fixed* set of 64 visual tokens: a small stack of Transformer blocks in which a set of 64 learned latent queries attend (cross-attention) to the flattened visual features, with the latents concatenated to the keys and values. Fewer visual tokens means cheaper cross-attention in the LM, and the fixed count makes the LM's input independent of resolution and video length. Temporal position embeddings are added for video.

**Gated cross-attention-dense layers (Figure 4).** Between the frozen LM blocks (Chinchilla 1.4B, 7B, 70B), insert new blocks that let text attend to the visual tokens $$X$$:

$$
y \leftarrow y + \tanh(\alpha_{\text{xattn}}) \cdot \operatorname{Attention}\big(q = \operatorname{LN}(y),\ kv = X\big), \qquad y \leftarrow y + \tanh(\alpha_{\text{dense}}) \cdot \operatorname{FFW}\big(\operatorname{LN}(y)\big)
$$

The scalar gates $$\alpha$$ are learned and **initialized to zero**, so at the start of training the new layers output exactly zero and the LM is untouched; the model then opens the gates gradually (Figure 6). This is what keeps the LM's language ability intact and makes training stable. Only the Resampler and these layers are trained (about 10B of the 80B parameters in the largest model).

**Interleaved inputs and masking (Figure 7).** A web page becomes a sequence of text with `<image>` tokens where images occurred. The visual tokens of all images sit alongside, and the cross-attention is *masked* so that each text token attends only to the visual tokens of the **single image that precedes it** in the sequence (the model is still aware of earlier images indirectly, through the LM's self-attention over earlier text). This per-image causal structure is what makes few-shot prompting with several (image, text) pairs work.

## Training objective

Maximize a weighted sum of per-dataset likelihoods of text given the preceding text and images,

$$
\sum_{m=1}^{M} \lambda_m\ \mathbb{E}_{(x, y) \sim D_m}\Big[-\sum_{\ell=1}^{L} \log p\big(y_{\ell} \mid y_{<\ell},\ x_{\le \ell}\big)\Big]
$$

over three sources: **M3W** (43M web pages with interleaved images and text, the crucial one), **ALIGN** (1.8B image–text pairs) plus LTIP (312M higher-quality pairs), and **VTP** (27M short videos with text). Gradients are accumulated across datasets rather than mixing them into one batch, which the ablations (Table 3) show matters, as does the interleaved data and the zero-initialized gating.

## Few-shot evaluation

A prompt is built by interleaving $$k$$ support (image, text) pairs with the query image, e.g. "`<image>` Output: A cat. `<image>` Output: A dog. `<image>` Output:", then decoding; for closed-form tasks, candidate answers are scored by likelihood. No parameters change.

## Results

Across 16 benchmarks (Figure 2, Table 1), Flamingo-80B with 32 shots beats the previous *fine-tuned* state of the art on 6 (VQAv2 among them is close), and with 4 shots is above all prior few-shot/zero-shot results everywhere. Performance improves with model size and with the number of shots (Figure 2 right); fine-tuning Flamingo (Table 2) sets new records on 5 more benchmarks.

## Thoughts

- "Zero-initialized gates" is the trick to remember: add a new pathway that starts as the identity and let training decide how much to use it. It reappears in adapters, ControlNet and many fine-tuning methods.
- Cross-attention is expensive to add to every LM layer; BLIP-2 moves the bridging *before* the LM (query tokens as a prefix) and LLaVA reduces it to a linear projection. Flamingo's design is the most flexible, theirs the cheapest.
- Interleaved web data is the multimodal analogue of GPT-2's WebText: the format of the data, not the architecture, is what teaches in-context learning.
