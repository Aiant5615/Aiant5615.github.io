## One-line summary

Encode a token's absolute position by *rotating* its query and key vectors by an angle proportional to the position, so that the dot product between a query at position $$m$$ and a key at position $$n$$ depends only on $$m - n$$. Absolute information goes in, relative information comes out, no extra parameters, and it works with linear attention.

## Why it matters

The original Transformer *added* sinusoidal vectors to the embeddings; T5 added learned relative biases to the logits. RoPE is the position scheme in LLaMA, PaLM, GPT-NeoX, Qwen, Mistral and most models since, and its structure (rotation frequencies per dimension pair) is what later context-extension methods (NTK scaling, YaRN) manipulate. Qwen2-VL's M-RoPE, reviewed in the multimodal series, extends it to three axes.

## The derivation

Self-attention scores are $$q_m^{\top} k_n$$ where $$q_m = f_q(x_m, m)$$ and $$k_n = f_k(x_n, n)$$ are functions of the token embedding and its position. The paper asks for functions such that the inner product depends on the embeddings and the *relative* position only:

$$
\big\langle f_q(x_m, m),\ f_k(x_n, n) \big\rangle = g(x_m, x_n, m - n)
$$

**The 2-D case.** Treat a 2-dimensional vector as a complex number. Then the solution (Section 3.4.1) is a rotation by angle $$m\theta$$,

$$
f_q(x_m, m) = (W_q x_m)\, e^{i m \theta}, \qquad f_k(x_n, n) = (W_k x_n)\, e^{i n \theta}, \qquad g(x_m, x_n, m - n) = \operatorname{Re}\big[(W_q x_m)(W_k x_n)^{*} e^{i (m - n)\theta}\big]
$$

because multiplying a query rotated by $$m\theta$$ against a key rotated by $$n\theta$$ leaves a net rotation of $$(m - n)\theta$$: absolute angles cancel, the difference survives. In matrix form, with the 2×2 rotation matrix $$R_{m}$$, $$f_q(x_m, m) = R_m W_q x_m$$.

**General $$d$$.** Split the $$d$$-dimensional vector into $$d/2$$ pairs and rotate each pair by its own angle $$m\theta_i$$:

$$
f_{\{q,k\}}(x_m, m) = R^{d}_{\Theta, m} W_{\{q,k\}} x_m, \qquad R^{d}_{\Theta, m} = \operatorname{diag}\big(R(m\theta_1), R(m\theta_2), \dots, R(m\theta_{d/2})\big), \qquad \theta_i = 10000^{-2(i-1)/d}
$$

The frequencies $$\theta_i$$ follow the original sinusoidal schedule: the first pair rotates fast (period $$2\pi$$ positions), the last very slowly, so together they encode position like a multi-hand clock. Because rotations are orthogonal, the norm of $$q$$ and $$k$$ is unchanged and the attention logit becomes

$$
q_m^{\top} k_n = \big(R^{d}_{\Theta, m} W_q x_m\big)^{\top}\big(R^{d}_{\Theta, n} W_k x_n\big) = x_m^{\top} W_q^{\top} R^{d}_{\Theta, n - m} W_k x_n
$$

using $$R_m^{\top} R_n = R_{n - m}$$. The relative position enters *multiplicatively* inside the dot product, unlike additive schemes, and only $$q$$ and $$k$$ are rotated, not $$v$$.

**Implementation (Figure 1).** The block-diagonal matrix is never built; for a vector $$x$$ with per-position cosines and sines,

$$
R^{d}_{\Theta, m}\, x = x \odot \begin{pmatrix} \cos m\theta_1 \\ \cos m\theta_1 \\ \vdots \end{pmatrix} + \operatorname{rotate\_half}(x) \odot \begin{pmatrix} \sin m\theta_1 \\ \sin m\theta_1 \\ \vdots \end{pmatrix}
$$

where $$\operatorname{rotate\_half}$$ swaps and negates each pair ($$(x_1, x_2) \to (-x_2, x_1)$$). Two element-wise products and an add.

**Long-term decay (Section 3.4.3, Figure 2).** Writing the logit as a sum over pairs and applying Abel's summation, the paper bounds the score by a term that decreases with $$\lvert m - n \rvert$$ for the sinusoidal $$\theta_i$$: far-apart tokens get systematically smaller attention, an inductive bias language likes. Figure 2 plots this relative upper bound falling with distance.

**Linear attention.** Since rotation is applied to $$q$$ and $$k$$ before the kernel feature map and preserves norms, RoPE can be inserted into Performer-style linear attention, which additive relative biases cannot.

## Results

- **Machine translation (WMT14 En–De):** RoFormer 27.5 BLEU vs 27.3 for the vanilla Transformer, a small gain.
- **Pretraining (Figure 3 left):** BERT-base with RoPE ("RoFormer") reaches lower masked-LM loss faster than BERT with absolute positions; **GLUE** fine-tuning wins on 3 of 6 tasks.
- **Performer (Figure 3 right):** on Enwik8 with linear attention, RoPE converges faster and lower.
- **Long documents (Chinese CAIL2019-SCM):** RoFormer at 1024 tokens beats WoBERT at 512 by 1.5 points, showing the model uses the longer context.

## Thoughts

- The clean part of the idea: absolute positions are stored *in the vector's angle*, and the dot product only sees angle differences. That is why RoPE needs no learned parameters and no bias tables.
- The base 10000 and the per-pair frequencies define the longest period the model has seen; extending context beyond training length is a matter of rescaling those frequencies, which is the entire content of several later papers.
- LLaMA (Part 7) and Qwen2-VL use exactly this scheme; the M-RoPE variant simply assigns different position ids to different groups of pairs.
