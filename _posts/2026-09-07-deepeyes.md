## One-line summary

Let a VLM's chain of thought contain images. DeepEyes wraps Qwen2.5-VL-7B's native grounding ability as an internal tool: at any point in its reasoning the model can output a bounding box, the crop is appended to the context as new image tokens, and reasoning continues. No workflow, no separate detector, no SFT on reasoning traces: the behaviour is trained with [GRPO](/blog/deepseekmath-grpo/) from outcome rewards on about 47K question–answer pairs, with a **conditional tool bonus** (only when the answer is correct) and a data-selection pipeline that keeps problems where zooming helps. The result improves high-resolution benchmarks by large margins (V* 71.2% → 90.1%, HR-Bench 8K +7.3), and also grounding, hallucination and math, and the paper shows the tool use evolving through exploration, heavy use, and finally selective use.

## Why it matters

OpenAI's o3 had shown "thinking with images" but not how. DeepEyes is the open recipe: treat the crop operation as an action in an agentic RL loop, mask the observation tokens out of the loss, and shape the reward so the model learns *when* to look closer. It moves this series from reasoning *about* an image to reasoning *with* it, and its data and reward design are the parts most later papers copy.

## Interleaved multimodal CoT (Section 3.1, Figures 1–2)

Given a question and image $$I_0$$, the model writes a text reasoning segment, then decides either to answer or to **zoom in** by emitting a list of boxes (`{"bbox_2d": [x1, y1, x2, y2], "label": …}`). The crops $$I_{t_1}, I_{t_2}, \dots$$ are returned, encoded by the same vision encoder, and appended; the model can zoom as many times as needed (up to 6 in training) before giving `<answer>`. Five claimed advantages over workflow methods (SEAL, DyFo, ZoomEye) and over text-only CoT: training needs only Q–A pairs; the policy generalizes because it *decides* when and where to look; the whole trajectory is optimized jointly; visual and textual evidence are interleaved; and the tool is the model's own grounding, so it can be improved implicitly by RL.

## Agentic RL formulation (Section 3.2)

In ordinary text CoT the state is the prompt plus all generated tokens. With tools, **observation tokens** from the environment (here, crop image tokens) are inserted into the rollout. The state at step $$t$$ is

$$
s_t = \{(X_0, I_0), (X_1, I_1), \dots, (X_t, I_t)\} = \{X_{\le t};\ I_{\le t}\}
$$

with $$X$$ the text segments and $$I$$ the images (interleaved), and the action $$a_t \sim \pi_{\theta}(a \mid s_t)$$ is the next token. The episode ends at an answer or at the zoom limit.

**Reward.** Sparse, trajectory-level:

$$
R(\tau) = R_{\text{acc}}(\tau) + R_{\text{format}}(\tau) + \mathbb{1}\big[R_{\text{acc}}(\tau) > 0\big]\, R_{\text{tool}}(\tau)
$$

Accuracy for a correct final answer (open-ended, judged by a model), a format term for well-formed output, and a **tool bonus paid only if the answer is correct and at least one zoom occurred**. Conditioning on correctness is what stops the model from zooming gratuitously: Figure 4 and Table 5 show that an unconditional tool reward inflates tool calls without helping accuracy, while no tool reward leaves the model reluctant to use it.

**Optimization.** GRPO on the whole multi-turn trajectory, with a token-wise loss mask so that the observation tokens (crops, tool-call scaffolding not produced by the model) contribute no loss. KL coefficient 0, 256 prompts × 16 rollouts per batch, 80 iterations, responses up to 20,480 tokens.

## Data curation (Section 3.3)

Three sources: the V* training set (fine-grained, small-object perception), ArxivQA chart questions (diversity), and ThinkLite-VL (hard reasoning). Filters, applied in order:

1. **Difficulty.** Run Qwen2.5-VL-7B several times; drop questions it always gets right or never gets right.
2. **Open-ended conversion and verification.** Rewrite to open-ended form; remove mislabeled items.
3. **Perception utility.** Keep only fine-grained items that become solvable when the ground-truth region is provided as a crop, i.e. items where active perception has a payoff. This filter is what makes RL from scratch (no SFT cold start) sample-efficient enough to work.

## Results (Section 4)

- **High resolution (Table 1).** V* overall 90.1 (+18.9 over Qwen2.5-VL-7B), beating workflow methods SEAL 75.4, DyFo 81.2, ZoomEye 90.6 and the 32B Qwen2.5-VL (87.9); HR-Bench 4K 75.1 (+6.3), 8K 72.6 (+7.3).
- **Real-world perception and reasoning (Table 2).** MME-RealWorld-Lite 53.2 vs 42.3 for the 7B base and 45.6 for the 32B; the largest gains are on remote sensing and autonomous driving sub-tasks.
- **Grounding and hallucination (Table 3).** refCOCO/+/g and ReasonSeg improve slightly; POPE 85.9 → 87.7, with Figure 5 showing why: the base model hallucinates "rocks" on a beach from linguistic association, DeepEyes zooms on the relevant region and reports the clock that is actually there.
- **Reasoning (Table 4).** MathVista 70.1 (+1.9), WeMath 38.9 (+4.3), consistent small gains across six math/logic benchmarks.
- **Ablations (Tables 6–9).** A 32B model trained the same way is stronger; more hard reasoning data improves both perception and math together; the tool generalizes zero-shot to rotated-OCR variants; the same data with text-only CoT is clearly worse than iMCoT.

## Training dynamics (Section 4.3, Figure 3)

Three stages on V*: (1) **initial exploration**, few and poorly placed zooms; (2) **frequent, effective use**, zoom count and response length spike while grounding IoU climbs; (3) **efficient exploitation**, zooms per question fall back while accuracy keeps rising, i.e. the model learns to zoom only when it needs to. Four recurring patterns emerge (Figures 7–10): visual search for small objects, visual comparison across regions, visual confirmation to remove uncertainty, and hallucination mitigation by inspecting details.

## Thoughts

- The formulation is small and clean: tool outputs are observation tokens, mask them from the loss, reward the outcome. What makes it *work* is the reward gating and the perception-utility filter, both of which are about making the tool worth learning in the early, noisy phase of RL.
- Using the model's own grounding as the tool is the elegant part: no second model, and the grounding itself gets better because it is inside the optimized trajectory (Table 3).
- The reasoning is still textual between looks; the crop is evidence, not a thought. The next paper goes further and asks whether the thoughts themselves can be images.
