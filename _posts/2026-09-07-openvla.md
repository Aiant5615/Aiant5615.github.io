## One-line summary

Reproduce the RT-2 idea with open components: start from a 7B vision-language model (Llama 2 with a fused DINOv2 + SigLIP vision encoder), express robot actions as 256-bin tokens overwritten onto the rarest tokens of Llama's vocabulary, and fine-tune on 970k episodes from Open X-Embodiment. The result outperforms the 55B RT-2-X by 16.5 points across 29 real-robot tasks, fine-tunes to new robots with LoRA on a single GPU, and runs at 6 Hz in 4-bit.

## Why it matters

RT-2 was closed and cloud-served. OpenVLA made a VLM-based VLA something anyone could download, fine-tune and run, and its careful evaluation against Octo and RT-2-X established the modern comparison set. It is also a reference point for the engineering series: LoRA and 4-bit quantization (QLoRA-style) applied to robotics.

## Model (Figure 2)

**Backbone.** Prismatic-7B: a **fused vision encoder** (SigLIP for semantics + DINOv2 for spatial detail, features concatenated channel-wise, 224×224 input → 256 patch tokens), a 2-layer MLP projector, and the Llama 2 7B language model. The DINOv2 half is credited with better spatial reasoning, which matters for manipulation.

**Action representation.** As in RT-2, each of the 7 action dimensions ($$\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z$$, $$\Delta$$roll, $$\Delta$$pitch, $$\Delta$$yaw, gripper) is discretized into **256 bins**; bin edges are placed uniformly between the **1st and 99th percentile** of each dimension in the training data rather than min/max, so outliers do not waste resolution. Llama's tokenizer reserves only 100 special tokens, so the **256 least-used tokens** of its 32k vocabulary are overwritten with the action bins. A training example is (image, "What action should the robot take to {instruction}?") → 7 action tokens, trained with next-token cross-entropy on the action tokens only.

**Training.** 970k trajectories from the OXE mixture (Octo's data mix, adjusted: single-arm end-effector-controlled datasets, DROID added then down-weighted), 27 epochs on 64 A100s over 14 days (21.5k A100-hours), batch 2048, constant learning rate $$2 \times 10^{-5}$$, full fine-tuning of all parameters including the vision encoder, which an ablation shows is important for OXE data. Input resolution stays 224 px (higher resolution did not help but tripled compute).

## Efficient adaptation and inference (Section 5.3–5.4)

- **Fine-tuning:** full fine-tuning, last-layer-only, frozen vision encoder, sandwich (vision + projector + last layer), and **LoRA** ($$r = 32$$, 1.4% of parameters) compared on Franka tasks; LoRA matches full fine-tuning at 8× less compute and fits on one A100 in 10–15 hours; last-layer-only and frozen-vision underperform.
- **Quantization (Figure 6, Table 3):** int4 weight quantization via bitsandbytes keeps success rate essentially unchanged (71.3% vs 71.9% in bf16) while cutting memory to 7GB and reaching 6 Hz on an RTX 4090; int8 is slower because the kernels are not optimized.

## Results

- **Bridge V2 / WidowX (Figure 3):** 17 tasks across visual, motion, physical and semantic generalization plus language grounding; OpenVLA 70.6% average success vs 47.1% RT-2-X and 20.0% Octo; RT-1-X and Octo fail on language grounding, OpenVLA follows the instruction in scenes with distractors.
- **Google robot (Figure 4):** 12 tasks in and out of distribution; OpenVLA 71.3% vs 63.9% RT-2-X on in-distribution and slightly behind on out-of-distribution, with RT-2-X's semantic generalization credited to its web co-fine-tuning, which OpenVLA does not do.
- **New robot setups (Figure 5):** on Franka tasks with 10–150 demonstrations, fine-tuned OpenVLA beats Diffusion Policy trained from scratch on multi-task, language-conditioned tasks (about +20 points on average) while Diffusion Policy remains better on narrow, precise single tasks; fine-tuned Octo is below both.
- **Data and architecture ablations (Table / Section 5.1):** the fused SigLIP+DINOv2 encoder beats SigLIP alone; OXE data curation matters; training from a VLM vs from scratch is a large gap.

## Thoughts

- Compared with RT-2, OpenVLA drops web co-fine-tuning and is smaller, and loses a little semantic generalization for it; the paper is explicit that this is the trade.
- The discrete-token bottleneck remains: one action per forward pass, 256 bins, ~6 Hz. Octo's and π0's continuous heads are the answer, and OpenVLA's own follow-ups adopt action chunking and parallel decoding.
- What it established for the field is the *baseline setup*: OXE pretraining, WidowX + Google robot evaluations, LoRA fine-tuning to a new setup with ~100 demos.
