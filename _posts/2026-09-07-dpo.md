## One-line summary

The KL-regularized RLHF objective has a closed-form optimal policy. Substituting it into the Bradley–Terry preference model expresses the reward in terms of the policy, so the policy can be fit to preference pairs *directly* with a logistic loss: no reward model, no sampling during training, no PPO. It matches or beats PPO-based RLHF on sentiment, summarization and dialogue.

## Why it matters

After [Stiennon](/blog/stiennon-summarize/) and [InstructGPT](/blog/instructgpt/), RLHF meant "train a reward model, then PPO", which is expensive and finicky. DPO collapses the two stages into one supervised-looking loss and became the default alignment method for open models within months.

## Derivation, step by step

**1. The RLHF objective.** Given a reward $$r(x, y)$$ and a reference policy $$\pi_{\text{ref}}$$ (the SFT model), RLHF maximizes

$$
\max_{\pi}\ \mathbb{E}_{x \sim D,\ y \sim \pi(\cdot \mid x)}\big[r(x, y)\big] - \beta\, \mathbb{D}_{\text{KL}}\big[\pi(y \mid x)\,\Vert\,\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)\big]
$$

**2. Its optimal policy is known in closed form.** Writing the objective per prompt as a KL between $$\pi$$ and a distribution proportional to $$\pi_{\text{ref}} e^{r/\beta}$$, the maximizer is

$$
\pi_r(y \mid x) = \frac{1}{Z(x)}\, \pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)\, \exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta} r(x, y)\Big), \qquad Z(x) = \sum_y \pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)\, \exp\!\Big(\frac{1}{\beta} r(x, y)\Big)
$$

(Appendix A.1). This is useless on its own because $$Z(x)$$ sums over all possible responses.

**3. Invert it.** Take logs and solve for the reward:

$$
r(x, y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_r(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)} + \beta \log Z(x)
$$

The reward is the log-ratio of the optimal policy to the reference, up to a per-prompt constant.

**4. The constant cancels in comparisons.** The Bradley–Terry model says $$p^*(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) = \sigma\big(r^*(x, y_1) - r^*(x, y_2)\big)$$. Substituting the expression from step 3, $$\beta \log Z(x)$$ appears in both rewards and cancels:

$$
p^*(y_1 \succ y_2 \mid x) = \sigma\!\Big(\beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y_1 \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_1 \mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi^*(y_2 \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_2 \mid x)}\Big)
$$

The preference probability is now written purely in terms of the *policy*. "Your language model is secretly a reward model."

**5. Fit the policy by maximum likelihood on the preference data.** With a dataset of $$(x, y_w, y_l)$$ (chosen, rejected), the negative log-likelihood is the DPO loss:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}}(\pi_{\theta}; \pi_{\text{ref}}) = -\mathbb{E}_{(x, y_w, y_l) \sim D}\Big[\log \sigma\!\Big(\beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_w \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_w \mid x)} - \beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y_l \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y_l \mid x)}\Big)\Big]
$$

That is the whole algorithm: compute the four log-probabilities (two from the trainable model, two from the frozen reference), take the difference of differences, scale by $$\beta$$, push it through a sigmoid, and do gradient descent. No sampling from the model during training, no value function, no reward model to train separately.

## What the gradient does

Define the implicit reward $$\hat r_{\theta}(x, y) = \beta \log \frac{\pi_{\theta}(y \mid x)}{\pi_{\text{ref}}(y \mid x)}$$. Then

$$
\nabla_{\theta} \mathcal{L}_{\text{DPO}} = -\beta\, \mathbb{E}\Big[\underbrace{\sigma\big(\hat r_{\theta}(x, y_l) - \hat r_{\theta}(x, y_w)\big)}_{\text{weight: how wrong the implicit RM is}}\ \Big(\underbrace{\nabla_{\theta} \log \pi(y_w \mid x)}_{\text{raise chosen}} - \underbrace{\nabla_{\theta} \log \pi(y_l \mid x)}_{\text{lower rejected}}\Big)\Big]
$$

It increases the likelihood of the chosen response and decreases that of the rejected one, weighted by how much the implicit reward currently *misranks* the pair. The paper stresses that without this weighting (plain likelihood up / unlikelihood down) the model degenerates; the weighting is what a classifier-style loss buys.

## Theory (Section 5)

Two rewards that differ by a function of $$x$$ alone induce the same preference distribution *and* the same optimal policy; DPO's reparameterization picks, from each such equivalence class, the unique representative for which $$Z(x) = 1$$ (Lemma 1–2, Theorem 1), so nothing expressible is lost. Section 5.2 also explains why actor-critic RLHF is unstable: the standard PPO reward $$r - \beta \log \pi/\pi_{\text{ref}}$$ is DPO's implicit reward minus a baseline $$\beta \log Z(x)$$ that has to be estimated (with a value function) or the gradients have high variance.

## Results

- **IMDb sentiment (Figure 2 left).** With a ground-truth sentiment reward available, plot reward achieved vs KL to the reference for DPO, PPO, PPO with the true reward ("PPO-GT"), unlikelihood and preferred-FT. DPO dominates every method, including PPO with access to the ground-truth reward, at every KL.
- **TL;DR summarization (Figure 2 right).** GPT-4-judged win rate against human references vs sampling temperature. DPO reaches about 61% at temperature 0 vs 57% for PPO, and stays robust as temperature rises while PPO collapses.
- **Anthropic HH dialogue (Figure 3).** DPO is the only method that beats the chosen responses in the dataset; the right panel shows the win rate rising quickly during fine-tuning.
- **Generalization / human agreement (Tables 1–2).** DPO transfers to CNN/DailyMail out of distribution, and GPT-4 judgments agree with humans about as well as humans agree with each other.
- **Best-of-N (Figure 4).** Sampling 64–128 responses and picking the best by a reward model is a strong but expensive baseline; DPO matches it with one sample.

## Thoughts

- DPO does not remove the need for a reference policy or for $$\beta$$; it removes the need to *estimate* the reward and the partition function. The KL regularization is baked into the loss.
- The data must be *pairs of responses to the same prompt*; unlike PPO it cannot use scalar rewards or a rule-based verifier directly. That is exactly the gap GRPO fills for verifiable tasks.
- Offline DPO can drift into regions the reference never covered ("overoptimization" again); iterated/online DPO variants re-sample pairs from the current policy for the same reason Ziegler collected labels online.
