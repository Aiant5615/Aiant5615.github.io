## One-line summary

A diffusion model's sampler is a chain of Gaussian steps, so treat each denoising step as an action in a $$T$$-step MDP, give the reward $$r(x_0, c)$$ at the end, and run a policy-gradient method on the *exact* per-step log-likelihoods. This is **Denoising Diffusion Policy Optimization (DDPO)**. With a clipped importance-sampling estimator (the PPO trick) it fine-tunes Stable Diffusion for objectives you cannot write in a prompt: JPEG compressibility, a learned aesthetic score, and prompt–image alignment judged by a vision-language model, and it beats reward-weighted regression on every one of them.

## Why it matters

Before this paper "RL for diffusion" meant weighting the ordinary denoising loss by a reward, which is an approximation twice over (the loss is only a bound on the likelihood, and the bound is on the final sample, not on the steps). DDPO is the first clean statement that the denoising chain *is* an MDP with tractable transitions, which is the formulation every later method in this series (DPPO for robot policies, Flow-GRPO for flow models) builds on or reacts against. It also introduced the now-standard reward menu for text-to-image RL.

## Two ways to see denoising as an MDP (Section 4)

The pre-trained model is a conditional diffusion model $$p_{\theta}(x_0 \mid c)$$ trained with the usual DDPM loss

$$
L_{\text{DDPM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x_0, c),\, t \sim U\{0, T\},\, x_t \sim q(x_t \mid x_0)}\big[\lVert \tilde{\mu}(x_0, t) - \mu_{\theta}(x_t, c, t)\rVert^2\big]
$$

where $$\tilde{\mu}$$ is the posterior mean of the forward process. Sampling starts from $$x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$$ and applies the reverse kernel

$$
p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c) = \mathcal{N}\big(x_{t-1};\ \mu_{\theta}(x_t, c, t),\ \sigma_t^2 I\big)
$$

$$T$$ times. The RL objective is simply the expected reward of the final sample,

$$
J_{\text{DDRL}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{c \sim p(c),\ x_0 \sim p_{\theta}(x_0 \mid c)}\big[r(x_0, c)\big]
$$

for any black-box reward $$r$$ and any prompt distribution $$p(c)$$. The paper shows two MDPs whose optimal policy maximizes this, and they lead to two different algorithms.

**One-step MDP → reward-weighted regression (RWR).** State $$s = c$$, action $$a = x_0$$, policy $$\pi(a \mid s) = p_{\theta}(x_0 \mid c)$$, reward $$r(x_0, c)$$, then termination. The policy's likelihood is the *marginal* $$p_{\theta}(x_0 \mid c)$$, which is intractable, so one falls back on the DDPM loss weighted by a function of the reward. Two weightings are studied:

$$
w_{\text{RWR}}(x_0, c) = \frac{1}{Z}\exp\big(\beta\, r(x_0, c)\big), \qquad w_{\text{sparse}}(x_0, c) = \mathbb{1}\big[r(x_0, c) \ge C\big]
$$

The first is the classic exponentiated-reward weight (it approximately solves a KL-constrained policy improvement); the second is "keep only samples above a threshold and fine-tune on them", i.e. filtered fine-tuning. Both are run for multiple rounds of sample-then-train, which makes them online. The catch: $$L_{\text{DDPM}}$$ is a variational bound, not the log-likelihood, so weighting it by $$w$$ optimizes $$J_{\text{DDRL}}$$ only loosely.

**Multi-step MDP → DDPO.** State $$s_t = (c, t, x_t)$$, action $$a_t = x_{t-1}$$, policy $$\pi(a_t \mid s_t) = p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c)$$, deterministic transition to $$(c, t-1, x_{t-1})$$, initial state $$(c, T, x_T \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I))$$, and reward

$$
R(s_t, a_t) = \begin{cases} r(x_0, c) & t = 0 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
$$

The trajectory return equals $$r(x_0, c)$$, so maximizing $$J_{\text{RL}}$$ in this MDP is the same as maximizing $$J_{\text{DDRL}}$$. The gain is that each per-step policy is an isotropic Gaussian with known mean and variance, so $$\log \pi(a_t \mid s_t)$$ and its gradient are exact. (Notation note: the paper indexes time $$T \to 0$$ as denoising proceeds, so "$$t = 0$$" is the final, clean image.)

## The two DDPO estimators

With exact likelihoods, the policy gradient is a Monte Carlo sum over the denoising trajectory.

**DDPO-SF (score function / REINFORCE):**

$$
\nabla_{\theta} J_{\text{DDRL}} = \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{T} \nabla_{\theta} \log p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c)\ r(x_0, c)\Big]
$$

with trajectories sampled from the *current* $$\theta$$. Every step's log-probability gets the same scalar credit $$r(x_0, c)$$ (there is no per-step reward and no value baseline in the paper's statement; in practice the reward is normalized per prompt). Being on-policy, it allows exactly one gradient step per batch of samples, and sampling is the expensive part.

**DDPO-IS (importance sampling):**

$$
\nabla_{\theta} J_{\text{DDRL}} = \mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{t=0}^{T} \frac{p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c)}{p_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c)}\ \nabla_{\theta} \log p_{\theta}(x_{t-1} \mid x_t, c)\ r(x_0, c)\Big]
$$

with trajectories from $$\theta_{\text{old}}$$. Each step is reweighted by its likelihood ratio, so several optimization steps can be taken on one batch. The ratio becomes unreliable when $$\theta$$ drifts far from $$\theta_{\text{old}}$$, and the fix is the [PPO](/blog/ppo/) trust region implemented by clipping the ratio to $$[1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon]$$. This is the variant that wins in the experiments: same samples, more updates.

## Rewards (Section 5)

- **Compressibility / incompressibility.** Fix 512×512 resolution and use the JPEG file size (negated, or not) as reward. It is trivially computed and impossible to request through a prompt, because captions never mention file size.
- **Aesthetic quality.** The LAION aesthetics predictor, a linear head on CLIP embeddings trained on 176k human ratings (1–10). Since it is fit to human judgments, this is RLHF in the sense of [Christiano et al.](/blog/christiano-preferences/) with a fixed reward model.
- **Prompt–image alignment from a VLM (Figure 2).** Ask [LLaVA](/blog/llava/) "what is happening in this image?", then score the description against the prompt with BERTScore recall (the prompt is the reference, the description the candidate). No human labels are needed; the design mirrors RLAIF in [Constitutional AI](/blog/constitutional-ai/). Any question could be substituted, so the reward's expressiveness is bounded only by the VLM.

## Results (Section 6)

Base model: Stable Diffusion v1.4. Compressibility prompts are the 398 ImageNet animal classes; aesthetic prompts are 45 common animals.

- **Algorithm comparison (Figure 4).** Reward vs number of reward queries, because reward evaluation is the bottleneck in practice. DDPO beats both RWR variants on all three tasks, and DDPO-IS edges out DDPO-SF thanks to the extra optimization steps per batch. The two RWR weightings are comparable, so the sparse (filtering) one is preferable for simplicity.
- **What the model learns (Figure 3).** For aesthetics it converts photographs into painterly artwork; for compressibility it deletes backgrounds and smooths the foreground; for incompressibility it adds high-frequency noise and sharp edges. None of these strategies were specified.
- **Prompt alignment (Figure 5).** Prompts "a(n) [animal] [activity]" with activities riding a bike / playing chess / washing dishes. BERTScore rises steadily over 50k queries and the samples become faithful to the activity, adopting a cartoon style along the way (the paper's guess: such scenes exist mainly as illustrations in the pre-training data).
- **Generalization.** Fine-tuned models transfer to unseen animals and activities, and the appendix compares against the concurrent DPOK, which trains per prompt and adds a KL penalty; DDPO trains on hundreds of prompts at once.

## Thoughts

- The whole contribution is one observation, "the transition kernel is Gaussian, so use it", plus the discipline to compare against the obvious baseline (weighted regression) under an equal reward budget. It is also an honest picture of what RL fine-tuning does: it finds whatever the reward model rewards, including artifacts.
- The importance-sampling variant is [PPO](/blog/ppo/) with the denoising chain as the episode. Everything in the LLM RL series (KL to a reference, reward hacking, group baselines) reappears here almost unchanged, which is why Flow-GRPO could later port GRPO directly.
- The weak point is that the formulation is tied to a *stochastic first-order* sampler: the policy must have a density per step. That constraint is exactly what DiffusionNFT later removes by working on the forward process instead.
