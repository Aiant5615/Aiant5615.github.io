## One-line summary

Stop resizing every image to one fixed square. Let the vision encoder process images at their *native* resolution and aspect ratio, producing as many visual tokens as the image needs (from a few dozen to over ten thousand), and give the language model position information in three dimensions (time, height, width) so images and videos share one representation. The 72B model matches GPT-4o on document, math and video benchmarks.

## Why it matters

Every model before this fixed the number of visual tokens (Flamingo 64, BLIP-2 32, LLaVA 576) or hacked around it with crops. Qwen2-VL makes the token count a function of the input, which is what document reading, high-resolution grounding and long video need, and it does so with position encodings that generalize the LLM's own RoPE rather than a bolt-on. It is the design most 2025 open VLMs follow.

## Naive dynamic resolution (Figure 2)

The vision encoder is a 675M-parameter ViT (used at all model sizes). Two changes make it resolution-agnostic:

- Remove the absolute position embeddings and use **2D-RoPE** inside the ViT, so the attention sees relative row and column offsets between patches and can accept any grid.
- After the encoder, an MLP **merges each 2×2 group of adjacent patch tokens into one** token, cutting the count by four; `<|vision_start|>` and `<|vision_end|>` tokens delimit the sequence when it enters the LLM.

With 14-pixel patches, a 224×224 image becomes 256 patches → 64 visual tokens; a 1092×8204 document page (Figure 2's left example) becomes about 11k tokens, a small icon a handful. Images of different sizes are packed into one sequence during training and a `min_pixels`/`max_pixels` range bounds the token count for cost control (Figure 4 shows how performance on InfoVQA, HallBench, OCRBench and MMMU changes with `min_pixels`; more pixels help document tasks and saturate elsewhere).

## Multimodal rotary position embedding (Figure 3)

Standard RoPE rotates each query/key dimension pair by an angle proportional to a single 1-D position id. **M-RoPE** splits the rotary dimensions into three groups that carry three ids: **temporal**, **height**, **width**. For a text token all three ids are equal (and equal to the token's 1-D index), so text behaves exactly as in the base LLM. For an image, the temporal id is constant across the image, and height/width ids are the token's row and column in the merged grid. For a video, the temporal id increments per frame (3D-convolutional patches cover two frames each, so ids advance every two frames), and height/width ids are as for images. The next modality's ids start after the largest id used so far, plus one. Two consequences: the model reasons about *where* in the image a token is, not just its serial index; and because the largest position id grows much more slowly than the raw token count, the LLM extrapolates to long videos better (Table 8 ablates M-RoPE against 1D-RoPE and finds gains on video, and that RoPE-style extrapolation to 32k+ tokens works).

## Unified image and video

Videos are sampled at two frames per second, encoded with 3D convolutions over two-frame temporal patches (so a single image is treated as two identical frames), and their resolution is adjusted dynamically so that a video's total token count stays under 16,384. Images and videos therefore go through the same encoder, same merge, and same position scheme.

## Model sizes and training

Three sizes, all with the same 675M ViT: Qwen2-VL-2B (1.5B LLM), 7B (7.6B LLM), 72B. Three stages: (1) train the ViT only, on about 600B tokens of image–text data (captions, OCR, interleaved documents, grounding); (2) unfreeze everything and train on about 800B tokens including video and mixed data; (3) instruction tuning of the LLM on the ChatML format with dialogue, grounding (boxes as text coordinates), agent/tool-use and video data, with the ViT frozen. Grounding, document parsing, and function calling are trained as text-formatted tasks.

## Results

Qwen2-VL-72B (Table 1): DocVQA 96.5, InfoVQA 84.5, RealWorldQA 77.8, MTVQA 32.6 (multilingual OCR), MathVista 70.5, MMMU 64.5, MVBench 73.6, and 72.3 on EgoSchema for long video, matching or beating GPT-4o and Claude-3.5-Sonnet on most of these; behind on MMMU-style college-level reasoning. The 2B model is competitive with much larger earlier open models on OCR-heavy tasks. Ablations (Section 4.4) show dynamic resolution beats fixed resolution at the *same* average token budget, and that scaling the LLM helps reasoning while more data helps perception.

## Thoughts

- The two ideas are separable: dynamic token counts (an efficiency/perception knob) and M-RoPE (a way to keep positions meaningful across modalities). Together they replace both the fixed resampler and the grid hack.
- Token cost now scales with pixels; the 2×2 merge is the only compression, so a document page costs as much context as a long article. Later work (token pruning, learned pooling) targets this.
- With this paper the "multimodal LLM" is no longer a language model with an eye attached; it is one sequence model whose positions live in space and time, closing the loop with the first Transformer's sinusoidal positions.
