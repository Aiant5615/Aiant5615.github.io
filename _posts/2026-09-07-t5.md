## One-line summary

Cast every task, including classification and regression, as "feed text in, generate text out" so that one encoder–decoder Transformer, one loss and one decoding procedure cover all of them; then use that uniform setup to run a controlled study of pretraining objectives, architectures, datasets, transfer strategies and scale, and combine the winners into models up to 11B parameters that set records on GLUE, SuperGLUE, SQuAD and summarization.

## Why it matters

After [GPT](/blog/2026/09/07/gpt1/) and [BERT](/blog/2026/09/07/bert/), dozens of variants claimed improvements under different conditions. T5's value is method: hold everything else fixed and measure one change at a time. Its conclusions (encoder–decoder with span corruption, scale beats cleverness) and its corpus (C4) shaped the next generation.

## The text-to-text format

Inputs get a task prefix, outputs are strings:

- Translation: `translate English to German: That is good.` → `Das ist gut.`
- Classification (MNLI): `mnli premise: … hypothesis: …` → `entailment` (a model producing a string outside the label set counts as wrong; it never happened after training).
- Regression (STS-B): the similarity score is rounded to the nearest 0.2 and emitted as text, `3.8`.
- Span QA: the answer text is generated, not pointed at.

The decoder uses greedy decoding at test time; the loss is always maximum-likelihood cross-entropy on the target tokens with teacher forcing. Because the interface is text, tasks can be *mixed* in one batch by sampling examples proportionally (with a per-task cap to stop the huge translation sets from dominating).

## Architecture

The original [Transformer](/blog/2026/09/07/transformer/) encoder–decoder with small changes: layer norm without the additive bias, placed *before* each sub-block (pre-norm) with the residual added after; dropout everywhere; and **relative position biases** instead of sinusoids: attention logits get a learned scalar $$b_{ij}$$ that depends only on the offset $$j - i$$,

$$
\operatorname{softmax}\!\Big(\frac{q_i k_j^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}} + b_{\text{bucket}(j - i)}\Big)
$$

with offsets grouped into 32 logarithmically spaced buckets up to 128 and a shared table across layers (separate per head). No position embeddings are added to the tokens at all. The vocabulary is a 32k SentencePiece model trained on English, German, French and Romanian.

Baseline size matches BERT-base on each side: 12 blocks, $$d_{\text{model}} = 768$$, 12 heads of 64, FFN 3072, about 220M parameters (twice BERT-base because of the decoder, but similar compute since each side sees only its own sequence).

## The pretraining objective, worked through

Figure 2's example. Original text: *Thank you for inviting me to your party last week.* Randomly pick tokens to corrupt (15%), and collapse each run of consecutive corrupted tokens into a single **sentinel** token (`<X>`, `<Y>`, …), unique within the example:

- Encoder input: `Thank you <X> me to your party <Y> week.`
- Decoder target: `<X> for inviting <Y> last <Z>`

The model must emit the missing spans in order, delimited by the same sentinels, ending with a final sentinel. Compared with BERT-style masking this has two advantages: the decoder only generates the *missing* text, so targets are short and training is cheaper; and the format is generative, so the same model can later produce free text. Section 3.3's flowchart searches the design space and finds: denoising objectives beat prefix language modeling and deshuffling; replacing spans with single sentinels equals or beats predicting whole corrupted inputs at a fraction of the cost; a corruption rate of 15% and a mean span length of 3 are about right, and the results are fairly flat around them.

## Architectures and attention masks compared (Section 3.2)

Figure 3 draws three masks over a sequence: **fully-visible** (every position sees every position; the encoder), **causal** (position $$i$$ sees $$\le i$$; a language model), and **causal with prefix** (the prefix, i.e. the input, is fully visible and the rest is causal). Figure 4 shows the three architectures built from them: encoder–decoder, a plain decoder-only LM fed input-then-target, and a prefix LM that is a decoder-only model with the prefix mask. With parameter count and compute matched (including a variant that shares encoder and decoder weights), the encoder–decoder with a denoising objective wins across GLUE, SQuAD, SuperGLUE and translation (Table 2). Sharing weights costs little; halving the number of layers costs a lot.

## The rest of the study

- **Datasets (Section 3.4):** C4 (Common Crawl filtered to English sentences ending in punctuation, no boilerplate, no offensive words, deduplicated at the 3-sentence level; 750GB) vs unfiltered C4 vs RealNews vs WebText-like vs Wikipedia vs Wikipedia+TBC. In-domain data helps specific benchmarks (news for ReCoRD, books+Wikipedia for SuperGLUE), but repeating a small corpus many times hurts (Table 9, Figure 6: training loss keeps falling as the model memorizes, downstream scores fall).
- **Training strategy (Section 3.5):** fine-tuning all parameters beats adapter layers and gradual unfreezing; multi-task pretraining (mixing supervised tasks into pretraining) roughly matches pretrain-then-fine-tune only when followed by fine-tuning.
- **Scaling (Section 3.6):** given 4× compute, options are 4× the steps, 4× batch, 2× model and 2× steps, 4× model, or an ensemble of 4. Bigger models trained longer win (Table 13); ensembles are competitive but expensive.

## The final recipe and results

Span corruption with mean length 3, encoder–decoder, C4, 1M steps of batch $$2^{11}$$ sequences of 512 tokens (about 1 trillion tokens), multi-task pretraining with mixing, fine-tuning per task, beam search of 4 for long outputs. Five sizes: Small (60M), Base (220M), Large (770M), 3B, **11B** (encoder and decoder each 24 blocks, $$d_{ff} = 65536$$, 128 heads). Table 14: T5-11B reaches GLUE 90.3 (from 89.4), SuperGLUE 88.9 (from 84.6, close to the 89.8 human baseline), SQuAD EM 91.3 / F1 96.2, CNN/DM ROUGE-2 21.55; translation does not beat back-translation systems because C4 is English-only.

## Thoughts

- "Everything is text-to-text" is the interface later adopted by instruction tuning ([InstructGPT](/blog/2026/09/07/instructgpt/)) and by chat models generally; T5 got there via classification prefixes rather than natural instructions.
- The paper is honest that most of the architectural tweaks it tested did not matter, and that scale and data did. That conclusion, plus the released checkpoints and C4, is why it is so widely used.
- Encoder–decoder vs decoder-only remains an open trade-off; GPT-3 went decoder-only for simplicity and in-context learning, T5 shows encoder–decoder is stronger per parameter on understanding tasks.
