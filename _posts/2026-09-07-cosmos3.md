## One-line summary

A robot today stitches together a VLM to plan, a VLA to act and a video "world model" to imagine consequences. Cosmos 3 makes them one model: a **Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)** in which every decoder layer has two parameter sets, a **reasoner** tower that runs autoregressively over text and ViT-encoded images/video, and a **generator** tower that denoises VAE video latents, audio latents and **action tokens** with a rectified-flow objective. The two towers meet only in attention, where generator queries can see reasoner keys but not the reverse. Depending on which tokens are given clean and which are noisy, the same weights act as a VLM, a text-to-image or image-to-video generator, an audio-visual generator, a forward-dynamics simulator, an inverse-dynamics model, or a policy that predicts actions *and* the video they will cause. Two open models are released, Nano (16B, from Qwen3-VL-8B) and Super (64B, from Qwen3-VL-32B), plus post-trained variants that ranked first among open models on text-to-image and image-to-video and first as a policy on RoboArena.

## Why it matters

It is the end point of two threads in this series. From the VLA side ([π0](/blog/pi0/) → [π0.7](/blog/pi07/)): the action expert and the subgoal-generating world model become one network with shared representations. From the reasoning side ([Cosmos-Reason1](/blog/cosmos-reason1/)): the physical-AI reasoner is the AR tower that the generator conditions on. Whether a single omnimodal backbone is the right abstraction for embodied agents is an open question, but this is the most complete attempt at it.

## Encoders (Section 2.1)

- **Image and video, twice.** For understanding, a ViT (16×16 patches, 2×2 token merge, DeepStack feature aggregation, text timestamps interleaved with frames, as in Qwen3-VL), trained jointly. For generation, the frozen Wan2.2 video VAE (4× temporal, 32×32 spatial compression) followed by a linear projection.
- **Audio.** A frozen audio VAE, 25 tokens per second of 48 kHz stereo, linearly projected.
- **Action (Figure 3).** A unified representation across embodiments built from three geometric components: an **ego pose** (9-D: 3-D translation + 6-D rotation) for the agent's observation frame, **effector poses** (9-D each), and **grasp states** (15-D fingertip positions for hands, 1-D open/close for grippers). Poses are *pseudo-actions*, relative transforms between consecutive SE(3) states, $$\Delta T_t = T^{-1}_{t-1} T_t$$, which avoids controller-specific details; grasp states encode the current state directly. Vehicles and cameras are 9-D, egocentric humans 57-D, single-arm robots 10-D, dual-arm 20-D, humanoids 29-D. Each domain $$k$$ has its own input and output projections,

$$
z = W^{(k)}_{\text{in}} x + b^{(k)}_{\text{in}}, \qquad x = W^{(k)}_{\text{out}} z + b^{(k)}_{\text{out}}
$$

into and out of the shared $$d_{\text{model}}$$ space, and predicted 6-D rotations are projected back to SO(3) by SVD. A learnable modality embedding is added to every non-language token.

## Token arrangement and generation modes (Section 2.2)

Every task is one sequence: an **AR subsequence** (language tokens and ViT tokens, ending in EOS and a begin-of-generation token) followed by a **diffusion subsequence** (VAE image/video, audio, action), with clean conditioning tokens before noisy target tokens, ordered vision → audio → action. With $$S_{\text{AR}} = [l_1, \dots, l_n, \langle\text{EOS}\rangle, \langle\text{BOG}\rangle]$$ and tildes marking noisy tokens:

$$
S_{\text{T2I}} = [S_{\text{AR}}, \tilde{v}_1], \qquad S_{\text{T2V+Audio}} = [S_{\text{AR}}, \tilde{v}_{1:N}, \tilde{s}], \qquad S_{\text{V2V}} = [S_{\text{AR}}, v_{1:P}, \tilde{v}_{P+1:N}], \qquad S_{\text{Transfer}} = [S_{\text{AR}}, v^{\text{ctrl}}_{1:N}, \tilde{v}_{1:N}]
$$

Image-to-video is $$P = 1$$; video transfer conditions on a clean control video (depth, edges) and denoises the RGB video. Language-only mode uses just the AR part and behaves as a standard VLM. **Action modes (Figure 4)**: an action token $$a_t$$ sits between video tokens and represents the transition $$v_{t-1} \to v_t$$. *Forward dynamics*: clean actions, noisy future video. *Inverse dynamics*: clean video, noisy actions. *Policy*: both future video and actions noisy, so the model generates the intervention and its expected visual consequence together.

## Mixture-of-Transformers (Section 2.3, Figure 5)

Each decoder layer holds two full sets of parameters (LayerNorms, attention projections, MLP), both initialized from the same pre-trained VLM: the reasoner processes the AR subsequence, the generator the diffusion subsequence. They share one attention operator with **dual-stream joint attention**:

$$
O_{\text{AR}} = \text{Attn}_{\text{causal}}(Q_{\text{AR}}, K_{\text{AR}}, V_{\text{AR}}), \qquad O_{\text{DM}} = \text{Attn}_{\text{full}}\big(Q_{\text{DM}},\ [K_{\text{AR}}; K_{\text{DM}}],\ [V_{\text{AR}}; V_{\text{DM}}]\big)
$$

AR tokens attend causally to AR tokens only, so the VLM's text generation is untouched; diffusion tokens attend bidirectionally to everything, so generation is conditioned on the prompt and reasoning while the reasoner is never updated by generation. Outputs are next-token predictions on the AR side and velocities on the diffusion side.

## Multimodal position embedding (Section 2.4, Figure 6)

3-D MRoPE (temporal, height, width) as in Qwen3-VL for the AR tokens. For diffusion tokens, video tokens vary on all three axes with $$t$$ the latent frame index, images are single-frame videos, and audio and action tokens use the temporal coordinate only ($$h = w = 0$$); coordinates reset at the start of each vision segment so they are within-video coordinates, and a modality offset separates text and vision temporal ranges. Because video, audio and actions can be generated together at different rates, the temporal index is modulated by **absolute time**: the increment per frame is $$\delta_t = \text{TPS}_{\text{base}} / \text{TPS}$$ with $$\text{TPS}_{\text{base}} = 24/4 = 6$$ latent tokens per second, so equal real durations occupy equal position ranges at 16, 24 or 30 fps.

## Variants, data, training (Sections 2.5–4)

| Variant | Params | Base | LLM layers | Hidden |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Edge | 4B | 2B dense, from scratch | 28 | 2,048 |
| Nano | 16B | Qwen3-VL-8B | 36 | 4,096 |
| Super | 64B | Qwen3-VL-32B | 64 | 5,120 |

(Total parameters are about double the base LLM because each layer is duplicated across the two towers.)

- **Reasoner**: 22.0M pre-training samples (OCR-heavy image–text plus text) and 2.2M Physical AI SFT samples (half video–text: video QA, video reasoning, grounding, captioning, prompt upsampling) for robotics, driving and smart infrastructure; next-token prediction, all components trained jointly, 16k-token sequences, square-root per-token loss normalization.
- **Generator**: initialized from the trained reasoner weights, then a curriculum of image/video/audio pre-training at 256p–720p across five aspect ratios, **mid-training** that adds action data (8.4M episodes, 61.3K hours across vehicles, cameras, humans and robots) and transfer data, then **post-training** for text-to-image, image-to-video and robot policy. Objective: rectified flow, $$x_{\sigma} = \sigma \epsilon + (1 - \sigma) x_0$$, predict $$v^{*} = \epsilon - x_0$$ with a masked MSE (conditioning tokens excluded), per-modality noise sampling (logit-normal for image/audio/action, mode sampling for video) and a shifted schedule $$\sigma = s\bar{t}/(1 + (s - 1)\bar{t})$$.
- **Policy post-training on DROID** (Section 4.2.5): from the mid-trained Nano with a fresh action encoder/decoder, a wrist view over two external views on a 540×640 canvas, 32 future absolute joint positions at 15 Hz plus auxiliary RGB frames; inference with 4 diffusion steps, shift 5, CFG scale 3 with parallel guidance, video decoding skipped, served on two RTX Pro 6000s.

## Results (Section 6, Table 1)

- **Reasoning**: Super 73.7 general / 57.8 robotics / 62.6 smart-infrastructure / 79.3 driving, vs Qwen3-VL-32B 72.8 / 52.6 / 56.1 / 40.7 and Gemini 3.1 Pro 77.5 / 58.2 / 58.6 / 47.2.
- **Generation**: post-trained Super text-to-image 91.36 (Gemini 3 Pro Image 90.85, Qwen-Image 84.25); text-to-video 80.0 and image-to-video 82.8 (Veo 3.1 79.1 / 82.6); audio 7.31.
- **Action**: robot forward dynamics 26.0 (Ctrl-World 23.0); Nano-Policy-DROID 39.7 (π0.5 28.1 on the same protocol), ranked first on RoboLab, RoboArena and MolmoSpaces at submission time.
- Ablations in the appendices: the reasoner initialization helps the generator, synthetic datasets (physics, robot, driving, human, warehouse sims) improve domain accuracy, action modes reinforce each other, and generated video and actions are mutually consistent.

## Thoughts

- The MoT is a careful compromise: the reasoner stays a VLM (causal, untouched by generation), the generator gets everything it needs from the reasoner through attention, and both towers start from the same weights. That asymmetric attention rule is the whole architecture in one line.
- Treating actions as a first-class diffusion modality, with pseudo-actions defined by pose differences, is what makes vehicles, humans and robots share one generator; the action-mode figure is the clearest statement I have seen of "world model and policy are the same network with different masks."
- Open questions: the report is a systems paper, so per-component ablations are thin relative to its size, and the policy result is one benchmark family. But the direction, one backbone for perceiving, imagining and acting, is where the VLA line has been heading since π0.
