## One-line summary

A deep LSTM reads the source sentence into a vector, a second deep LSTM generates the target from it, and with a large model, beam search and one odd trick (feed the source backwards) the pure neural system outscores a mature statistical translation pipeline.

## Why it matters

[Cho et al.](/blog/rnn-encoder-decoder-gru/) used the encoder–decoder as a feature inside SMT. This paper is the first to show the neural model alone can win on a serious benchmark (WMT'14 English–French), which made "seq2seq" the default framing for translation, summarization, dialogue and, later, everything that a decoder-only Transformer does.

## The model

**Factorized likelihood.** For a source $$x = (x_1, \dots, x_T)$$ and target $$y = (y_1, \dots, y_{T'})$$ of different lengths, the encoder LSTM produces a fixed vector $$v$$ (its final hidden and cell state) and the decoder LSTM defines

$$
p(y_1, \dots, y_{T'} \mid x_1, \dots, x_T) = \prod_{t=1}^{T'} p\big(y_t \mid v, y_1, \dots, y_{t-1}\big)
$$

where each factor is a softmax over the target vocabulary computed from the decoder state. A special end-of-sentence symbol ⟨EOS⟩ terminates both sequences, which is what lets the model handle variable lengths. Training maximizes $$\frac{1}{\lvert S\rvert } \sum_{(T, S) \in S} \log p(T \mid S)$$ over the parallel corpus; decoding finds the most likely translation with a small left-to-right **beam search** (beam size 1 already works well; size 2 gives most of the benefit).

**The LSTM cell.** The paper cites Graves' formulation. Per time step, with input $$x_t$$ and previous state $$h_{t-1}$$,

$$
i_t = \sigma(W_{xi} x_t + W_{hi} h_{t-1} + b_i), \quad f_t = \sigma(W_{xf} x_t + W_{hf} h_{t-1} + b_f), \quad o_t = \sigma(W_{xo} x_t + W_{ho} h_{t-1} + b_o)
$$

$$
c_t = f_t \odot c_{t-1} + i_t \odot \tanh(W_{xc} x_t + W_{hc} h_{t-1} + b_c), \qquad h_t = o_t \odot \tanh(c_t)
$$

The cell $$c_t$$ is an additive memory: the forget gate $$f_t$$ scales the old memory and the input gate $$i_t$$ adds new content. Because the update is a sum rather than a nonlinearity applied to the whole state, gradients travel back through many steps, the same idea as the GRU's interpolation and, later, residual connections.

**Architecture and training.** Two different LSTMs (encoder and decoder), each with **4 layers** and 1000 cells per layer, 1000-dimensional embeddings, 160k source and 80k target vocabularies, and a 384M-parameter total. Weights were initialized uniformly in $$[-0.08, 0.08]$$; plain SGD with learning rate 0.7, halved every half-epoch after epoch 5, for 7.5 epochs; batches of 128; gradients rescaled when their norm $$s$$ exceeded 5, i.e. $$g \leftarrow 5g/s$$; and, importantly, sentences within a minibatch were sorted by length so that the padding waste dropped and training ran twice as fast. The whole thing ran on an 8-GPU machine, one layer per GPU plus four GPUs for the softmax, at about 6,300 words per second.

**Reversing the source.** The trick with the biggest effect: feed "C B A" instead of "A B C" while keeping the target in order. Perplexity dropped from 5.8 to 4.7 and BLEU rose from 25.9 to 30.6. The authors' explanation is about *minimal time lag*: after reversal the first source word is adjacent to the first target word, so the earliest decoder steps have short paths back to their relevant inputs, and SGD can "establish communication" early. Average distance between corresponding words is unchanged, but the many short-lag pairs matter more than the few long ones.

## Results

On WMT'14 English–French: an ensemble of 5 reversed deep LSTMs scored **34.81 BLEU** versus 33.30 for the phrase-based baseline, the first time a purely neural system beat SMT on this task. Using the LSTM to rescore the baseline's 1000-best lists gave 36.5, near the 37.0 state of the art of the time. A single 4-layer model scored 30.6 with beam 1 and 31.5 with beam 2. ## Thoughts

- The "flat BLEU vs length" curve is real but partly an artifact of the reversal trick and the 4-layer capacity. [Bahdanau et al.](/blog/bahdanau-attention/) show the fixed vector *does* hurt at 50+ words when the encoder is smaller, and attention removes the problem.
- Almost every engineering detail here became standard: EOS tokens, beam search, gradient clipping, length-sorted batches, model-parallel layers.
- The reversal hack is a symptom. The disease, that information has to travel through many steps, is what attention and then self-attention cure by giving every output direct access to every input.
