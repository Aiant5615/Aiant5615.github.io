## One-line summary

Put a small trainable Transformer, the **Querying Transformer** (Q-Former), between a frozen image encoder and a frozen LLM. A fixed set of learned query vectors extracts the visually relevant information into 32 output vectors, which are projected into the LLM's embedding space as a prefix. Two pretraining stages, 188M trainable parameters, and zero-shot VQA above Flamingo-80B with 54× fewer trainable parameters.

## Why it matters

[Flamingo](/blog/flamingo/) modifies the language model with cross-attention layers at every block. BLIP-2 leaves the LLM completely untouched and does all the bridging in a bottleneck *before* it, which is cheaper, lets you swap LLMs, and is the design LLaVA simplifies further to a single projection.

## The Q-Former (Figure 2)

Two Transformer submodules share self-attention layers: an **image transformer** that receives $$32$$ learnable query embeddings (dimension 768) and cross-attends to the frozen image encoder's output (ViT-L/14 from CLIP, or ViT-g/14 from EVA-CLIP, 257 patch features), and a **text transformer** that reads text tokens. Queries and text interact only through the shared self-attention, and *which* tokens can attend to which is controlled by a mask that changes per objective. Because the output is always 32 × 768 regardless of image resolution, the bottleneck forces the queries to keep only what the text side needs (compare Flamingo's 64-token Perceiver Resampler).

## Stage 1: vision-language representation learning

Train the Q-Former (image encoder frozen) with three objectives at once, on 129M image–text pairs, each with its own attention mask:

- **Image–text contrastive (ITC).** Align the query outputs $$Z$$ with the text `[CLS]` output $$t$$: similarity is the *maximum* over the 32 query–text similarities, and a symmetric contrastive loss with in-batch negatives is applied. Mask: **unimodal**, queries and text cannot see each other (no leakage).
- **Image-grounded text generation (ITG).** Generate the caption from the queries: text tokens attend to all queries and to earlier text, queries cannot attend to text. Mask: **multimodal causal**. This forces the queries to capture everything needed to write the caption, since text has no other route to the image.
- **Image–text matching (ITM).** Binary classification of whether a pair matches, computed from each query's output through a linear head and averaged; hard negatives are mined from the ITC similarities. Mask: **bi-directional**, everything attends to everything.

## Stage 2: vision-to-language generative learning (Figure 3)

Attach the Q-Former to a frozen LLM. A fully-connected layer projects the 32 query outputs to the LLM's input dimension and they are prepended to the text as **soft visual prompts**. For a decoder-only LLM (OPT, 2.7B–6.7B) the loss is the language-modeling loss on the caption; for an encoder–decoder LLM (FlanT5-XL/XXL) the caption is split into a prefix fed to the encoder with the visual tokens and a suffix generated by the decoder (prefix LM loss). Only the Q-Former and the projection are trained. Because stage 1 has already made the query outputs *language-informative*, the LLM receives inputs it can use, and the paper shows (Figure 5) that skipping stage 1 makes zero-shot VQA collapse and OPT training suffer catastrophic forgetting.

## Results

- **Zero-shot VQAv2:** 65.0% for ViT-g + FlanT5-XXL, above Flamingo-80B's 56.3%, with 188M trainable parameters vs 10.2B. Prompt: "Question: {} Answer:".
- Zero-shot image captioning (NoCaps CIDEr 121.6 after fine-tuning captioning), and fine-tuned image–text retrieval (Flickr30k, COCO) at state of the art with only the stage-1 model.
- Instructed zero-shot image-to-text generation (Figure 4): visual conversation, knowledge-based reasoning ("what dish is this and how is it made"), personalized captions, all by prepending text instructions after the visual prompt, inheriting the LLM's instruction-following.
- Ablations: ITG loss is the important one for stage 2; stronger image encoders and stronger LLMs both help (Table 1).

## Thoughts

- The Q-Former is a learned compression: 257 patches → 32 vectors. The number is a design choice that trades detail for cost; later work that needs OCR or fine detail (LLaVA-1.5-HD, Qwen2-VL) goes the other way and feeds hundreds or thousands of tokens.
- Freezing the LLM avoids catastrophic forgetting but caps how "visual" the LLM can become; LLaVA unfreezes it and finds that with instruction data the LLM adapts fine.
- Three objectives with three masks in one module is elegant but delicate; the surprising ablation is that ITG alone carries most of stage 2's benefit.
