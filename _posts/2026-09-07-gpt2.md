## One-line summary

A language model trained on enough diverse text learns many tasks *as a by-product* of predicting the next word; with 1.5 billion parameters and a new 40GB web corpus, GPT-2 reaches state of the art on 7 of 8 language-modeling benchmarks in a zero-shot setting and shows early reading comprehension, summarization and translation without any task-specific training.

## Why it matters

[GPT](/blog/2026/09/07/gpt1/) needed a labeled dataset per task. GPT-2 argues that the fine-tuning stage is optional if the pretraining corpus is large and varied, because the corpus already contains demonstrations of the tasks. This shifts the field's attention from architectures to *scale and data*, and sets up [GPT-3](/blog/2026/09/07/gpt3/).

## The framing: tasks as conditioning

A language model estimates $$p(x) = \prod_{i} p(s_i \mid s_1, \dots, s_{i-1})$$. A supervised system estimates $$p(\text{output} \mid \text{input})$$. A general system, the paper says, should estimate

$$
p(\text{output} \mid \text{input}, \text{task})
$$

and in natural language the task can be *stated in the input*: "translate to French, english text, french text" or "answer the question, document, question, answer". If a corpus contains such patterns (and web text does: quotes with translations, FAQ pages, summaries after articles), then minimizing next-token loss also minimizes these conditional losses. Nothing in the objective changes; the claim is about what the data contains.

## Data: WebText

Common Crawl is mostly junk, so the authors scraped all outbound links from Reddit posts with at least 3 karma (a cheap human-curation signal), ~45M links, deduplicated and filtered to 8M documents, 40GB of text, with Wikipedia removed to avoid test-set overlap.

## Input representation: byte-level BPE

Word-level vocabularies cannot cover arbitrary web text; character- or byte-level models score worse. GPT-2 runs [BPE](/blog/2026/09/07/bpe-subword/) on **bytes** (base vocabulary 256, so any string is encodable) rather than Unicode characters (base vocabulary 130k). One rule is added: merges are not allowed across character categories (letters, digits, punctuation, spaces), except that a space may attach to a following word, so "dog", "dog.", "dog!" do not each become separate tokens. Final vocabulary 50,257.

## Model

The [GPT](/blog/2026/09/07/gpt1/) decoder with three changes: layer normalization is moved to the **input** of each sub-block (pre-norm) and one extra layer norm is added after the final self-attention block; residual-path weights are scaled at initialization by $$1/\sqrt{N}$$ where $$N$$ is the number of residual layers (so the sum of $$N$$ residual contributions keeps unit variance); the vocabulary and context grow to 50,257 and 1024 tokens; batch size 512. Four sizes are trained (Table 2):

| parameters | layers | $$d_{\text{model}}$$ |
|---|---|---|
| 117M | 12 | 768 |
| 345M | 24 | 1024 |
| 762M | 36 | 1280 |
| 1542M | 48 | 1600 |

The smallest matches GPT, the second matches BERT-large, the largest is GPT-2.

## Zero-shot evaluation

**Language modeling (Table 3).** The model is evaluated on other datasets' text with *their* tokenization mapped through the byte-level BPE (detokenizers fix formatting quirks like `<UNK>`). GPT-2 sets new records on WikiText-2, PTB, enwik8, text8, WikiText-103 (17.5 perplexity, from 18.3) and LAMBADA (accuracy 63.2% from 59.2%; perplexity 8.6 from 99.8), and on the Children's Book Test (93.3% on common nouns). It loses only on the 1 Billion Word benchmark, whose sentence shuffling destroys the long context GPT-2 relies on.

**Tasks by prompting.**

- *Reading comprehension (CoQA):* condition on the document, the conversation so far and the token `A:`, decode greedily. 55 F1, matching 3 of 4 fine-tuned baselines without seeing any of the 127k training examples.
- *Summarization (CNN/DailyMail):* append `TL;DR:` and sample 100 tokens; ROUGE is only slightly above picking three random sentences, and removing the hint drops it further, so the model *understood* the hint but summarizes badly.
- *Translation:* prompt with a few `english sentence = french sentence` pairs and then `english sentence =`. 11.5 BLEU Fr→En, better than several unsupervised baselines, remarkable because WebText is filtered to English and contains only about 10MB of French.
- *Question answering (Natural Questions):* 4.1% exact match, but 63% on the 1% of questions the model is most confident about, and the smallest model gets under 1%, so factual recall scales with size.

## Figures, explained

- **Figure 1 (zero-shot performance vs parameters).** Four panels (reading comprehension, translation, summarization, QA), each plotting task score against model size on a log axis with horizontal lines for baselines. Every curve rises with size; only reading comprehension and translation reach competitive levels at 1.5B, and the trends suggest more capacity would keep helping.
- **Figure 2 (train/test perplexity on WebText vs size).** Both curves keep falling with model size and the gap between them stays small: even the 1.5B model *underfits* WebText. This is the plot that justified GPT-3.
- **Figure 3 (CBT accuracy vs size)** and **Figure 4 (LAMBADA perplexity vs size).** Straight improvement with scale on log-parameter axes; the CBT curve approaches the human level.
- **Table 4 (memorization check).** Percentage of 8-grams in each benchmark's test set that also appear in WebText, compared with overlap between each benchmark's own train and test sets. WebText overlap is real (1–13%) but generally below the benchmarks' own leakage, so the gains are not mostly memorization.

## Results

State of the art on 7 of 8 tested LM datasets zero-shot. Task performance improves log-linearly with parameters. Qualitatively, the samples (Table 13 in the appendix, the "unicorns in the Andes" article) are coherent across paragraphs, which was new at the time and is why OpenAI initially released only the smaller models.

## Thoughts

- The architectural changes are small; the paper's contribution is the evidence that *data plus scale* produces task ability without task supervision. The pre-norm change, however, is the version of the Transformer block that stuck.
- "Zero-shot by writing the task into the prompt" is prompting. GPT-2 does it awkwardly (`TL;DR:`); [GPT-3](/blog/2026/09/07/gpt3/) makes it the main interface and adds examples to the prompt.
- Figure 2 (underfitting at 1.5B) is a scaling-law argument in one plot, made before the scaling-law papers formalized it.
