## One-line summary

Cut an image into 16×16 patches, treat each patch as a token, and run an unmodified Transformer encoder over the sequence. With enough pretraining data (14M–300M images) this beats the best convolutional networks on ImageNet and transfer benchmarks at a fraction of the compute; with little data, it loses, because it has none of a CNN's built-in assumptions about images.

## Why it opens the multimodal list

CLIP, Flamingo, BLIP-2, LLaVA and Qwen2-VL all feed a language model with the output of a ViT. Knowing that "an image is a sequence of patch tokens" is what makes the later papers, which mostly decide *how* to connect that sequence to a text Transformer, easy to read.

## The model, equation by equation

Reshape an image $$x \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times C}$$ into $$N = HW / P^2$$ flattened patches $$x_p \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times (P^2 \cdot C)}$$ (for $$224 \times 224$$ with $$P = 16$$, $$N = 196$$ patches of $$768$$ numbers each). Project each patch to the model width $$D$$ with a learned matrix $$E$$, prepend a learnable class token, and add learned 1-D position embeddings:

$$
z_0 = \big[x_{\text{class}};\ x_p^1 E;\ x_p^2 E;\ \dots;\ x_p^N E\big] + E_{\text{pos}}, \qquad E \in \mathbb{R}^{(P^2 \cdot C) \times D},\ E_{\text{pos}} \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+1) \times D}
$$

The patch projection is a linear layer, equivalently a convolution with kernel and stride $$P$$; it is the *only* image-specific operation. Then $$L$$ standard pre-norm Transformer blocks:

$$
z'_{\ell} = \operatorname{MSA}\big(\operatorname{LN}(z_{\ell-1})\big) + z_{\ell-1}, \qquad z_{\ell} = \operatorname{MLP}\big(\operatorname{LN}(z'_{\ell})\big) + z'_{\ell}, \qquad \ell = 1 \dots L
$$

and the image representation is the class token's final state, $$y = \operatorname{LN}(z_L^0)$$, followed by an MLP head (one hidden layer for pretraining, a single linear layer for fine-tuning). Position embeddings are 1-D (no 2-D structure); the model learns 2-D-like similarity on its own (Figure 7 centre). Sizes: ViT-Base (12 layers, $$D = 768$$, 86M), Large (24, 1024, 307M), Huge (32, 1280, 632M); "ViT-L/16" means Large with 16-pixel patches.

**Inductive bias.** A CNN has locality, translation equivariance and a 2-D neighbourhood structure baked into every layer. A ViT has almost none: only the patch cut and the fine-tuning-time position interpolation are 2-D. The paper's thesis is that large-scale training beats inductive bias.

**Fine-tuning at higher resolution.** Keep the patch size, so the sequence gets longer; the pretrained position embeddings are 2-D-interpolated to the new grid. Higher resolution at fine-tuning time consistently helps.

**Hybrid.** Patches can be taken from a CNN feature map instead of raw pixels; hybrids win at small compute budgets and lose the advantage at large ones (Figure 5).

## Training and results

Pretrain on ImageNet (1.3M), ImageNet-21k (14M) or JFT-300M (303M) with Adam, then fine-tune with SGD at 384–512 resolution. Table 2: ViT-H/14 pretrained on JFT reaches **88.55% ImageNet top-1**, 90.72% ReaL, 94.55% CIFAR-100 and 77.63% VTAB, above BiT-L (ResNet152x4) and Noisy Student, using roughly a quarter of the pretraining compute.

## Thoughts

- Everything after this paper treats images as tokens. The engineering questions that remain (how many tokens per image, at what resolution, and how to fuse them with text) are exactly what the next six papers vary.
- The "no inductive bias" bet paid off only with big data; CLIP's 400M pairs and later web-scale corpora are what make ViT the default.
- Patch size is a compute knob: $$P = 14$$ at 336 px gives 576 tokens, the number LLaVA-1.5 feeds to its LLM; Qwen2-VL merges 2×2 patches to cut that by four.
